10th August 2026 - (Hong Kong) Hong Kong Customs intercepted a suspected drug trafficking attempt at Hong Kong International Airport today, seizing about 4 kilograms of suspected cannabis buds valued at roughly HK$0.9 million from an incoming passenger's check-in suitcase.

Officers made the discovery during Customs clearance after a 28-year-old Mainland man arrived from Bangkok, Thailand. The consignment had been concealed in his checked baggage, and he was arrested on the spot once the suspected cannabis buds were found. Investigators say the case centres on baggage concealment rather than body packing or cargo consignments, underlining how traffickers still try to move controlled substances through ordinary passenger luggage on regional flights.

Customs described the operation as a drug trafficking case detected through airport screening and risk-based selection. The department said enquiries remain under way, with no further details released on whether the man was acting alone or whether other parties outside Hong Kong may be linked to the shipment. Airport arrivals from higher-risk routes continue to draw closer scrutiny as officers weigh travel patterns, declarations and baggage profiles.

The department pledged to keep intensifying enforcement against drug trafficking by using intelligence analysis to target suspicious movements. Officials also issued a public warning: people should stay alert and refuse to join trafficking schemes for money, must not accept hiring or delegation from anyone to carry controlled items into or out of Hong Kong, and should never carry unknown packages for others. Those messages are aimed at both residents and travellers who may be approached with offers that sound lucrative but expose them to serious criminal liability.

Customs said it will continue applying a risk assessment approach and focusing clearance resources on passengers arriving from high-risk regions, seeking to disrupt transnational trafficking chains before consignments reach the street market. The strategy pairs intelligence leads with selective secondary checks rather than relying solely on random inspection, reflecting the volume of traffic through Hong Kong International Airport and the need to concentrate effort where concealment is most likely.

Under the Dangerous Drugs Ordinance, trafficking in a dangerous drug is a serious offence. On conviction, the maximum penalty is a fine of HK$5 million and life imprisonment. Cannabis remains a controlled dangerous drug in Hong Kong, and moving suspected buds in commercial quantities through the airport attracts the full trafficking framework rather than a mere possession narrative.

Anyone with information about suspected drug trafficking is urged to tip off Customs through its 24-hour hotline 182 8080, the dedicated crime-reporting email account crimereport@customs.gov.hk, or the online form at eform.cefs.gov.hk/form/ced002. The seizure of about 4 kilograms with an estimated market value of about HK$0.9 million, the arrest of the 28-year-old arrival from Bangkok, and the continuing investigation form the core of today's airport case as Customs presses its wider campaign against cross-border drug movements.
