11th August 2026 - (Hong Kong) Former Radio Television Hong Kong assistant director Brian Chow Kwok-fung has bought a newer Discovery Bay flat for HK$6.5 million after selling his previous home in the same district.

Market sources said the purchase was Amalfi Phase 16, Block 5, a mid-floor unit B with a saleable area of 551 square feet in a one-bedroom layout. The asking price had been HK$7 million last year before the vendor cut it to HK$6.5 million last month, implying about HK$11,797 per square foot. The flat still has a sitting tenant, and Chow is understood to have bought without viewing the property in person.

Chow, who has become better known recently as a judge on the television programme Midlife, Sing!, previously sold a two-bedroom home at Habitat, Coastal Zone Two, Discovery Bay. Land records and market reports put that earlier buy at HK$4.947 million in 2014 for a 600-square-foot high-floor unit A at La Vista/Habitat, which changed hands last month for HK$5.7 million. After about 12 years he booked a paper profit of roughly HK$753,000 before moving into the newer Amalfi block.

The Amalfi vendor had bought around 2021 for about HK$8.136 million and exited after five years with a paper loss of about HK$1.636 million. That contrast — a celebrity buyer trading up within Discovery Bay while an earlier owner cuts losses — fits a second-hand market that has now risen for 13 consecutive months, with more public names reported to be rotating homes while prices firm.

Chow's public profile stretches well beyond property. A former disc jockey and 1980s Educational Television child performer, he later led RTHK Radio 2 and the Putonghua channel and served as assistant director of broadcasting for radio and programme planning before leaving in 2021. Those credentials, plus the Midlife, Sing! judging role, have kept his transactions in the local property gossip circuit.

The same report also flagged a separate Tseung Kwan O deal at East Point City. A low-floor two-bedroom unit H in Block 3, saleable area 524 square feet, sold for HK$7.15 million, or about HK$13,645 per square foot. The buyer company Rising Excellence Ltd lists directors including names associated with Great Eagle Holdings (00041) management. The previous owner had held since about 1997 after paying roughly HK$3.459 million, leaving with a paper gain near HK$3.691 million after 29 years.

Together the Discovery Bay switch and the East Point City trade show how both lifestyle estates and new-town mass flats are seeing active turnover. For Chow, the story is a same-district upgrade into a younger block at a reduced asking price; for the wider market, it is another data point in a 13-month second-hand climb where named buyers are willing to move quickly — even sight-unseen — when the numbers fit.
