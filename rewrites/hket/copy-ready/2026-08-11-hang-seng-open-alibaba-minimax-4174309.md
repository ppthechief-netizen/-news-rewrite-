11th August 2026 - (Hong Kong) The Hang Seng Index opened 61 points higher at 25,998 on Tuesday morning, pressing towards the 26,000 mark with early turnover of about HK$2.59 billion.

The Hang Seng Tech Index rose 0.3 per cent to 4,932 at the open, putting technology and internet names at the centre of the first print. Alibaba Group Holding (09988) gained about 2 per cent, NetEase (09999) advanced roughly 2 per cent, and newly listed artificial-intelligence name MiniMax (00100) climbed more than 4 per cent, while peer Zhipu (02513) added about 3 per cent in early dealing.

Not every heavyweight joined the advance. AIA Group (01299) fell nearly 2 per cent against the broader tape, and XL Southern Hynix (07709) continued to open about 4 per cent lower. The mixed tape underlined how leadership remained concentrated in selected China internet and AI-model stocks rather than a uniform blue-chip rally.

Overnight futures had already pointed to a firmer cash open. Hang Seng Index futures were up about 51 points near 9.10am, quoted around 25,997, after the night session closed 17 points higher at 25,966. The Chinese ADR basket rose about 1.7 per cent to 6,730, and Alibaba's US listing traded roughly 2 per cent above its Hong Kong close, supporting local sentiment into the morning auction.

Exchange-traded ADR indications before the cash open showed Meituan about 0.59 per cent higher on a Hong Kong-dollar converted basis, Tencent about 0.26 per cent firmer and HSBC about 0.24 per cent softer, while Alibaba's US shares were indicated about 2.42 per cent above the local close. Those signals helped frame the early leadership in internet names once the cash market began.

Separately, MiniMax was flagged as meeting the Hong Kong Exchanges and Clearing Technology 100 Index fast-inclusion pathway, with membership due after the 12th August close and effective from 13th August. The timetable added a technical catalyst for a stock already leading the early percentage gainers among AI-model listings.

Corporate news flow also featured Television Broadcasts, with reports that the group and Gaw Capital were discussing a joint venture to build artificial-intelligence computing facilities. Broader morning briefings highlighted large US artificial-intelligence infrastructure financing plans and rising humanoid-robot shipment forecasts that kept technology themes in focus for Hong Kong traders.

Wall Street had finished mixed overnight. Unresolved talks over Hormuz shipping access and Intel's plan to raise about US$15 billion through a new share issue weighed on sentiment: the Dow fell 60 points to 53,975, the S&P 500 eased four points to 7,753 and the Nasdaq slipped 0.32 per cent to 26,605. Oil and gold were firmer in the same session, but US equity softness limited any risk-on follow-through into Asia.

For Hong Kong, the early session therefore combined a technical test of 26,000, AI-model stock strength, selective insurance weakness and a still-cautious US backdrop. Live updates through the day will show whether the opening bid holds once turnover builds beyond the first HK$2.59 billion print.
