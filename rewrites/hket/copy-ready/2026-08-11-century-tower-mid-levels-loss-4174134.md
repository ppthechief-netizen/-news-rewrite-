11th August 2026 - (Hong Kong) A four-bedroom whole floor at Century Tower in Mid-Levels has changed hands for HK$86.8 million, crystallising a paper loss of about HK$18.2 million for an owner who bought near the 2018 peak.

Market sources said the deal covered a low-floor whole floor in Block 2 with a saleable area of 2,792 square feet. The vendor paid about HK$105 million in 2018 and has now exited at roughly HK$31,089 per square foot, implying a 17 per cent fall over eight years. The trade shows that even after a multi-month second-hand recovery, some peak-cycle buyers in the luxury segment are still forced to book steep mark-to-market cuts when they sell.

A second loss-making house deal was flagged in Tai Po. Century 21 Pak Shek Kok branch director Lo Ka-lun said Beverly Hills has seen livelier trading this month as estate owners add holdings. A five-bedroom house on the even-number side of Lake View Road, saleable area 1,902 square feet, was offered at HK$19.8 million and sold after negotiation for HK$18.48 million, or about HK$9,716 per square foot. The seller had bought around 2011 for about HK$20.88 million and left after roughly 15 years with a paper loss of HK$2.4 million, or an 11 per cent decline.

Lo also said Beverly Hills recorded four lettings this month, with monthly rents ranging from HK$42,000 to HK$90,000. That rental activity sits alongside the ownership loss on the house sale, underlining how New Territories luxury stock can still turn over even when capital values for long-held inventory remain underwater versus earlier purchase prices.

In Sai Kung, Century 21 Kee Fung sales director Liu Chun-hung reported the first Choi To Villa trade in nearly four years. An even-number house at Silver Lake Road with 1,789 square feet of saleable area and a private garden of about 2,052 square feet, looking over Silverstrand Bay, sold for HK$50 million, or about HK$27,949 per square foot on the saleable area. The asking price had been HK$58 million; the final cut of HK$8 million, or 13.8 per cent, was described as in line with market clearing levels.

Taken together, the Mid-Levels, Tai Po and Sai Kung tickets sketch a second-hand luxury pocket where prices have recovered from the trough yet still leave 2018-era and earlier peak buyers nursing losses. Vendors who need to exit are trimming asks — sometimes sharply — while buyers bargain for discounts that keep paper write-downs in the tens of millions at the top end and the low millions for New Territories houses.

For the wider Hong Kong residential tape the message is two-sided: liquidity is returning in named estates, but the path of least resistance for high-basis owners remains a negotiated loss rather than a clean break-even. That pattern is likely to keep surfacing in Mid-Levels towers and outlying luxury houses until more peak-vintage stock is cleared.
