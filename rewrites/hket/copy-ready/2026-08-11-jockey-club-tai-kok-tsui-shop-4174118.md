11th August 2026 - (Hong Kong) The Hong Kong Jockey Club has sold a long-held Tai Kok Tsui retail block for HK$51 million, with a Christian church group taking the vacant premises for its own use.

Land and transaction records show the property at Pak Shing Building, 27-41 Tong Mi Road, covers about 3,497 square feet on the ground floor and about 3,540 square feet on the first floor — more than 7,000 square feet in total. The deal was struck vacant possession at roughly HK$7,200-plus per square foot across the floor area.

The Jockey Club bought the shop in 1987 for HK$7 million and ran it for years as an off-course betting branch. The branch closed around the middle of last year and the space stayed empty while the owner marketed it for lease and for sale. After 39 years the club leaves with a profit of about HK$44 million, or a gain of roughly 6.3 times the original outlay.

Industry background helps explain the disposal. The club still operates on the order of 100 off-course branches, most in buildings it owns, but mobile and online wagering has reduced the need for street-level counters. Only a limited number of branches have closed in recent years, and outright sales have been rare; the previous comparable in the same district was in 2017, when the club sold vacant shops at 197-199 Tong Mi Road for HK$39.5 million. That earlier parcel of about 3,070 square feet had been held for 42 years and rose about 17.7 times in value.

The buyer is named as Hong Kong Christian Shuen Tao Church, a religious body with a long presence in Tai Kok Tsui. Market understanding is that the group intends to convert the dual-floor shop into a self-use meeting place rather than hold it as an investment letting. A church taking a former betting hall underlines how large street-level floorplates in older Kowloon streets can find new owner-occupiers when traditional retail or wagering uses fade.

For the commercial property tape the sale is notable less for the absolute price than for the vendor and the vacancy story. A rare Jockey Club freehold disposal, a long empty shop after a branch closure, and an end-user community buyer together sketch a niche corner of Hong Kong's street-shop market where yield investors may pass but institutions with a local mission still write large cheques.

Settlement on a vacant basis also means the church can plan fitting-out without waiting out a tenant, while the club crystallises a multi-decade capital gain on a site whose operating role has diminished. Further branch reviews will be watched to see whether more owned outlets follow the same path from betting counter to alternative owner-use.
