11th August 2026 - (Hong Kong) A 43-year-old cabin cleaner died after collapsing while cleaning a passenger aircraft at Hong Kong International Airport last Friday morning, Hong Kong Aircraft Engineering Company Limited has confirmed.

At 10.24am on 7th August the male worker boarded an airliner cabin for cleaning and suddenly fainted. Colleagues discovered him and called for help at once. The man was an employee of HAECO, the airport maintenance group formally known as Hong Kong Aircraft Engineering Company Limited.

HAECO said it was deeply saddened by the death of its colleague. He became unwell and lost consciousness while carrying out cabin cleaning on an aircraft at Hong Kong International Airport. The company said the cabin temperature was maintained at 24 degrees Celsius when the incident happened.

He was taken to hospital but later died. HAECO extended deep condolences to his family, friends and colleagues, adding that the matter is under investigation and that the group is actively cooperating with the relevant departments. It would make no further comment at this stage.

No aircraft registration, airline customer or medical diagnosis was released with the company's reply. The confirmed 24-degree cabin reading answers one immediate question about heat inside the aircraft without settling the medical cause of death, which investigators have yet to publish.

Cabin cleaning is turnaround work finished between flights in confined aisles and galleys, often under tight ground-time pressure before the next boarding call. When a worker collapses on duty, the public record so far runs from the 10.24am boarding and faint, through the colleague alert and hospital transfer, to HAECO's confirmation of death and its pledge to assist investigators.

Until the inquiry concludes, HAECO's public statement remains limited to the collapse during cabin cleaning, the confirmed cabin temperature, its condolences and its cooperation with the authorities — leaving unanswered why a worker in a temperature-controlled cabin lost consciousness and did not survive, and whether any workplace factors beyond the stated temperature will feature in later findings.
