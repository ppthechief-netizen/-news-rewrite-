10th August 2026 - (Hong Kong) Sham Shui Po's Bishop Hill — officially Woh Chai Shan — long tangled in nearly two decades of informal occupation after fitness gear and even a giant Buddhist altar appeared from about 2009, and famous since 2020 when demolition threats to its century-old Romanesque service reservoir sparked heritage outcry, is now under a government upgrade that will clear unsafe and unlawful structures before installing safer recreation.
The Home Affairs Department said on social media the plan serves residents' overall interest by upgrading recreation and rest facilities and fixing safety hazards for a "double upgrade" of gear and safety, so people can play table tennis and exercise in a safer, more comfortable setting. Works of different types are expected to finish from late August into September. After clearance and unsealing, seven packages follow at suitable safe spots: repairing wayfinding signs; placing proper seats instead of sharp-tile makeshift benches some perched on slope edges; upgrading shade shelters with seats, roofs and foundations; mending cut railings on paths; replacing uneven old fitness equipment with padded safer sets; repairing and protecting individual slopes and levelling ground; and adding table-tennis tables on the hill and at Tai Hang Tung Playground.
