10th August 2026 - (Hong Kong) Sham Shui Po's Bishop Hill — officially named Woh Chai Shan — became widely known in 2020 when demolition work around its service reservoir nearly destroyed a century-old Romanesque structure, but unlawful occupation of government land there has tangled for nearly 20 years. From about 2009 residents began placing all sorts of fitness gear themselves, including exercise bikes, and some even set up a giant Buddhist altar on their own initiative.

The Home Affairs Department said on its social-media page that the Woh Chai Shan upgrade plan starts from residents' overall interest: it will improve recreation and rest facilities on the hill and fix places with safety hazards, delivering a "double upgrade" of facilities and safety. Once the works are finished, people will be able to play table tennis, exercise and rest on Woh Chai Shan in a safer, more comfortable setting, giving district residents a better recreation and leisure experience.

Because the upgrade packages are advancing at different speeds, the government hopes the various items can be completed from late August into September. After the Home Affairs Department finishes clearance work, the government will unseal the site and immediately push ahead at suitable and safe spots on Woh Chai Shan with seven types of recreation, rest and slope works.

Those packages are: (1) repairing wayfinding and directional signs; (2) placing safe, comfortable seating in secure positions to replace makeshift benches piled from sharp tiles and other materials, some of them perched on slope edges; (3) upgrading existing shade shelters, for example by adding seats and repairing roofs and foundations; (4) repairing railings on hillside paths that have been cut; (5) replacing a series of new fitness facilities with safety mats, in place of the present worn, uneven sets that lack soft padding; (6) carrying out repair and protection on individual slopes and levelling the ground so people can move nearby safely; and (7) adding more table-tennis tables at safe positions on the hill and at Tai Hang Tung Playground.
