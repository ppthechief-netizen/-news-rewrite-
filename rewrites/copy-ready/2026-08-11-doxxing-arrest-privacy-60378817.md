11th August 2026 - (Hong Kong) A 37-year-old mainland man who supplies frozen meat to restaurants has been arrested on suspicion of doxxing a former restaurant owner and a female debt mediator after unpaid bills, the Office of the Privacy Commissioner for Personal Data said.

Police acting on a complaint from the Privacy Commissioner for Personal Data's Office detained the supplier on suspicion of breaching section 64(3A) of the Personal Data (Privacy) Ordinance by disclosing personal data on social media without the two data subjects' consent. He has been released on bail pending further investigation, and the watchdog said inquiries continue.

The supplier ran a wholesale food business. In March 2025 he met a male restaurant operator through a consultancy run by a female intermediary and delivered frozen meat to the eatery from March to May that year. The restaurant later fell behind on payments. The supplier discussed repayment with the male owner but no agreement was reached, and the restaurant closed in late May 2025.

About a year later, in late May 2026, a social media account published a post accusing the male owner of unpaid debts and urging netizens to locate him, accompanied by negative comments. The post disclosed his English name, several schools he had attended, a vehicle registration number, personal photographs, a former company name and its registered address. It also carried photos of the man and his family and screenshots of his former company's registration records.

A day later the same account posted again, alleging that the female intermediary owed money. That post published her English alias, telephone number and the name of her consultancy company. The Privacy Commissioner for Personal Data's Office reminded the public not to doxx others over money disputes, saying the practice does not resolve conflicts and often escalates them.

The watchdog stressed that doxxing is a serious offence. Offenders convicted under the relevant provision face a maximum fine of HK$1 million and imprisonment for five years, and the office urged residents not to test the law. Investigators are still tracing how the account obtained the disclosed records and whether further posts targeted either data subject after the two May 2026 messages appeared online.
