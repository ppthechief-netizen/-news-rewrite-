10th August 2026 - (Hong Kong) With Chief Executive John Lee Ka-chiu preparing his fifth policy address — public consultation opened on 29th June alongside views on Hong Kong's first five-year plan — HK01 has been consolidating and updating a thick political and business wishlist spanning consumption vouchers, housing sales, labour rules, family subsidies, bridge tolls, aviation, space-industry offices, elderly health care and small-claims reform.

The Democratic Alliance for the Betterment and Progress of Hong Kong, which failed to revive the Tenants Purchase Scheme last year, is back: flats aged about 20 to 30 years could be priced around HK$1 million each. DAB vice-chairman and Hong Kong Island West legislator Chan Hok-fung says more than 80 per cent of residents in his constituency would buy their own public flats, helping households settle while lifting fiscal reserves by more than HK$10 billion, with profits he says could last more than 20 years — and he wants the measure written into this year's address.

The Federation of Trade Unions wants a Building Maintenance Authority to lead tendering, bid evaluation, valuation and works supervision, piloting 39 Tenants Purchase Scheme estates to break systemic bid-rigging on large repairs. Separately the FTU seeks an aid fund for families after workplace sudden deaths, citing research that more than 840,000 people die worldwide each year from work-related disease, with long hours and high stress among the main drivers. Liberals propose electronic cash vouchers for next year's 30th anniversary of the handover — HK$100 off every HK$300 spent — to lift local retail and catering, plus a domestic-helper expense tax deduction and progressive child allowances to encourage births.

After consultation opened, the Business and Professionals Alliance met Mr Lee and tabled about 130 proposals across nine areas. On cross-boundary transport, vice-chairman Lau Ip-keung pressed lower Hong Kong-Zhuhai-Macao Bridge tolls: private cars can still pay more than RMB300 for a round trip except on designated free days, so he wants monthly and annual passes, simpler applications and, longer term, study of extending "Guangdong cars south" toward northern mainland plates. Party leader Ng Wing-ka noted airline capacity has recovered to more than 90 per cent of pre-pandemic levels and wants more routes and frequencies to move people, goods and capital for the Greater Bay Area and Belt and Road. New People's Party chairwoman Regina Ip Lau Suk-yee again seeks a space-economy co-ordination office, filled within six months by open recruitment of people who know the sector, and a fourth harbour tunnel from Tseung Kwan O to Hong Kong Island to ease three near-saturated crossings.

Cross-party C15+ lawmakers want the public-housing rent-to-sale mix shifted from today's 6:4 toward 5:5, trialled first in the Northern Metropolis, with 95 per cent mortgages for eligible young buyers there to aid upward mobility, plus a means-test-free HK$2,500 student grant restored in the next financial year. Federation of Hong Kong and Kowloon Labour Unions chairman Lam Chun-sing would improve the re-employment subsidy pilot along the two-tier model of the middle-aged employment scheme — up to HK$15,000 after six months' full-time work for those aged 60 or above, and another HK$15,000 after twelve, for a maximum HK$30,000. Colleague Chow Siok-chung wants second-tier imported-labour triggers keyed to vacancy, wage and unemployment data by trade, a HK$100 extra levy per foreign worker on employers under a "support local unemployed workers" charge, and a catering transition fund financed from that levy for conditional short-term unemployment aid to jobless catering workers.

G19 urges rent-to-buy trials on subsidised Northern Metropolis homes for young buyers, and for public tenants' asset and income checks a data-driven approach that studies rolling average income — for example over 12 months. For key firms landing in the north it wants competitive land and tax incentives, flexible site sizes and research space so companies are not lost in supply gaps, plus diversified financing including "land for industry" collaboration, Northern Metropolis special bonds and offshore renminbi tech debt. It also seeks a wider Cross-boundary Payment Connect daily ceiling of HK$50,000. Convenor Kenneth Fok Kai-kong, from the sports, performing arts, culture and publication sector, wants Victoria Park's tennis courts rebuilt as a 10,000-seat multi-purpose all-weather arena to attract higher-tier international events.

Election Committee member Fan Kai-kit would make newborn bonuses permanent and raise them to HK$30,000. New Territories associations' Tam Chun-kwok seeks a restored HK$2,500 means-test-free student grant to ease every family's back-to-school costs, and a carefully priced Tenants Purchase Scheme revival on suitable estates with a repair reserve and a sale ban period. Koon Ho-ming wants the monthly HK$1,000 childcare-centre parent subsidy for dual-income or grassroots families raised, centre hours extended to 8.00pm, weekend and emergency temporary care added, progressive child allowances that climb from a second child, and grandparent carer subsidies or tax relief. He also wants elderly health vouchers lifted toward HK$3,000 a year with personal account caps of HK$10,000, usable for private physiotherapy and mental-health care, and a health savings account that can take part of MPF or public subsidy for preventive care such as annual check-ups and vaccines. The FTU further wants elderly vouchers usable at more Fujian and Hainan clinics under a multi-point city model, plus Macau-style subsidies for Hong Kong people buying mainland medical insurance. Maggie Chan Man-ki proposes lifting the Small Claims Tribunal ceiling from HK$75,000 to HK$100,000, a foreign domestic helper employment tax deduction, and a bidding law to strike hard at collusion, bid-rigging and bribery.
