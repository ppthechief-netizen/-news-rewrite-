10th August 2026 - (Hong Kong) Hong Kong Trade Development Council chair Professor Frederick Ma Si-hang, GBS, JP said he believes China will win its artificial intelligence contest with the United States, even as he expects an AI investment bubble to burst.

US-China rivalry has spread from trade into technology, including AI. After earlier talks in Beijing, President Xi Jinping is expected to visit the US next month for another meeting with President Trump. Mr Ma said rivalry will not stop, and one or two summits will not suddenly make the two sides "old mates", so global markets still face instability — which he argued can favour Hong Kong.

As a free port with a unique location, he said, Hong Kong benefits when there is contention or unrest abroad. He cited Middle East conflict driving capital once aimed at that region into Hong Kong, and firms using the city as a business platform amid geopolitics.

Against the US-China trade war backdrop, Hong Kong's imports and exports rose about 40 per cent in the first half, helped by AI-related semiconductor demand, lifting the city to the world's fifth-largest trading port ahead of many developed economies.

On AI competition, Mr Ma contrasted heavy US research spending with China's ability to deliver results with less outlay, citing DeepSeek. He compared electric vehicles, where mainland brands started later yet BYD leads global sales, and high-speed rail beyond 50,000 kilometres, the world's longest. "Whatever China does well — EVs, robots, high-speed rail — I have confidence in the country," he said, adding China can beat the US and be the winner in the tech and AI race.

Recent swings in South Korea's market, including wild moves in SK Hynix, have fuelled bubble talk. JPMorgan Chase chief executive Jamie Dimon has repeatedly warned that the AI boom and high-risk leverage evoke 2008 or the late-1990s tech bubble, with over-optimism, geopolitics, fiscal deficits and heavy cloud borrowing as risks.

Mr Ma said his view is similar to Mr Dimon's: the AI investment "bubble will burst", and US stocks look loftier than Hong Kong's. Yet past cycles show markets recover slowly after corrections because demand returns, so he does not see the AI trend ending soon.

On Hong Kong equities lagging Wall Street and other markets, he said US and Korean shares drew huge inflows; Korea has nearly collapsed at times, while US stocks have not swung as violently but carry much more risk than before. Hong Kong, he argued, has been steadier with less risk.
