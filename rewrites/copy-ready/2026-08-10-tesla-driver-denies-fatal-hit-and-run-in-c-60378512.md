10th August 2026 - (Hong Kong) A woman driving a Tesla through Cheung Sha Wan is accused of continuing after a South Asian motorcyclist fell in a collision and then leaving the scene, with the rider later dying of his injuries. She later contacted police and faces three charges, including dangerous driving causing death and failing to stop after an accident (DCCC379/2025).

Today, 10th August, in the District Court she admitted one count of using glass that did not comply with safety regulations and denied the other two charges as the trial opened. A video-recorded interview played in court captured her telling officers that at the time she thought she had run over some other object; after going home and seeing news that someone had left without stopping, she wondered whether that person was herself and then contacted police of her own accord.

The defendant, Lau Siu-lai, was 65 at the time of the alleged offences. She is charged with dangerous driving causing death, failing to stop after a traffic accident, and using glass that did not meet safety rules. The indictment alleges that on 6th March 2024 on Lin Cheung Road in Cheung Sha Wan, Kowloon, she dangerously drove a private car and caused the South Asian victim's death.

Opening for the prosecution, counsel said the crash happened at about 10pm on 6th March 2024 in light rain on a flat road with three traffic lanes. The victim was riding a motorcycle. The defendant was driving her Tesla Model X. Nearby were also a taxi, a white Toyota and another motorcycle.

The victim was travelling in the first lane; the taxi was in the second lane behind him. He later changed lanes until his machine was almost parallel with the taxi and his brake lever struck the taxi's door, yet the taxi did not stop. Soon afterwards he lost balance and, with his bike, hit the white Toyota in the third lane, sending sparks across the road. The Toyota driver steered and braked at once, but the victim was thrown onto the carriageway.

The defendant's vehicle then ran over the victim, yet she did not stop and left. He was taken to Caritas Medical Centre and was certified dead that same evening. The autopsy recorded a laceration of the left lower limb and a pelvic fracture, and found that death was caused by this incident.

In the early hours of the following morning the defendant dialled 999 and later told police her private car might be linked to the case. Officers eventually arrested her for dangerous driving causing death and failing to stop after an accident.
