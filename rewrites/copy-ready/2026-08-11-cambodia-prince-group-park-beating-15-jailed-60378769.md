11th August 2026 - (Phnom Penh) A Cambodian court has sentenced 15 supervisors and security staff at a Prince Group compound near the Cambodia-Vietnam border to 13 years in prison each after a fatal assault on four Chinese men who tried to flee the sealed site, leaving one dead and three seriously injured.

The violence unfolded on 29th December 2025 at the conglomerate's Jinyun compound in Kandal province, close to the frontier with Vietnam. Four Chinese nationals attempted to climb over a closed metal gate to escape but were set upon by 15 compound managers and guards. Closed-circuit footage shown in court captured the men struggling to walk after clearing the gate, only to be beaten repeatedly with sticks and pipe-like weapons on their limbs, torsos and heads while others blocked their path. After they were subdued, the escapees were dragged back inside.

On 6th August the Kandal Provincial Court announced its verdict. The prosecutor confirmed the 15 defendants included one Chinese man, Le Heneng, and 14 Cambodian men. The single fatality and three seriously wounded victims were all Chinese. On 31st July the court reviewed the CCTV and other key evidence before convicting all 15 of aggravated intentional violence committed by multiple persons and aggravated intentional violence causing death, handing down 13-year prison terms to each.

Prince Group Holdings is one of Cambodia's largest conglomerates, controlling more than 80 subsidiaries including Prince Real Estate Group, Prince Bank and Cambodia Airways. The group has repeatedly been linked to criminal activity. With support from Cambodian authorities, China's Ministry of Public Security has this year repatriated group chairman Chen Zhi and senior figures including Li Xiong and Liu Ren, underscoring Beijing's parallel crackdown on scam compounds that have drawn international sanctions and indictments.

The Jinyun case is one of several violent episodes associated with fortified labour compounds operated under the Prince umbrella, where workers have reported coercion, confinement and brutal punishment for those who resist or flee. The prison terms mark a rare local prosecution of front-line enforcers even as investigations continue against the network's leadership.
