10th August 2026 - (Hong Kong) Under peripheral sinking air from Super Typhoon White Dolphin, the Hong Kong Observatory headquarters logged a maximum of 36.9C on Sunday, 9th August — the highest since records began in 1884 — leaving streets furnace-hot and turning ordinary fans and portable rechargeable fans into everyday cooling tools.

Looking up the Consumer Council site, Choice magazine's July 2025 issue 585 set out six safe-use tips and five care secrets for portable charging fans, including the warning that leaving a lithium battery at high charge for long periods speeds ageing, so packs should not be topped up to full whenever circumstances allow.

On circulating claims that blowing a fan at the face for long periods may raise facial-palsy risk, neurosurgeon Dr Tse Yat-hang told that Choice issue the medical community has reached no settled conclusion. Some research links prolonged exposure of the face to cold air with palsy, yet cold air is generally defined as below 15C, so any link may turn more on a sudden temperature drop than on ordinary fan breeze.

Dr Tse added that studies suggest the face may need hours of cold-air exposure before palsy risk rises, and that people with chronic illnesses such as diabetes may face higher risk. The Consumer Council reminds users that if any cooling product leaves them unwell they should stop and seek medical help when needed.

The council's six safe-use tips are: choose reputable brand-original fans, batteries, chargers and cables; tie long hair back before use and keep hair or clothing clear of the blades; let children use fans only under adult watch and never as toys, taking special care with neck cords that can strangle if misused; avoid damp settings so moisture cannot seep in and cause damage or danger; do not knock, drop or crush the fan, battery or charger; and stop at once if the fan, battery, charger or cable deforms, breaks or overheats, unplugging the charger safely.

Its five charging and storage tips are: do not charge unsupervised overnight or while asleep — keep the unit in sight and unplug or switch off after charging; avoid habitually charging lithium cells to full because sustained high charge ages them faster; place the fan in a cool, dry, ventilated spot when charging or storing, away from clothes, flammables, clutter, direct sun or anything that blocks heat dispersal; if the fan or battery will sit unused for a long stretch, discharge or charge to a mid-level state then power off; and every few months take an idle pack out and restore it to mid charge because natural drain otherwise leaves cells unhealthy.

Together the six use rules and five care rules make the eleven practical points Choice urged readers to review as extreme heat returns.
