11th August 2026 - (Hong Kong) A young kitten trapped in a drain outside Chun Ma House at Ma Hang Estate in Stanley was rescued yesterday afternoon after police, firefighters and Society for the Prevention of Cruelty to Animals staff answered a call at about 3pm on 10th August.

Rescuers heard mewing in the drain but could not see the cat at first. Neighbours put food at the opening and eventually drew the "greedy" kitten out; SPCA officers lifted it and a resident took it for care.

The SPCA said firefighters, police and an SPCA inspector found a young male local shorthair with no injuries, later handed to an SPCA volunteer on scene. Photos in a Stanley community group showed officers lying flat at the drain mouth searching for the animal.

A resident who then took the kitten said it was a male about six weeks old. Estate security had seen it running in planters the day before, apparently separated from its mother and fallen into the drain; neighbours heard cries from the pipe that morning.

The narrow drain defeated early pulls by fire and police. The resident put kitten food at the mouth and played mother-cat sounds until it peeked out, then an SPCA inspector used a snare to lift it. She later said it had been fed and people could rest easy.

Estate managers often warn residents to cover open drains and report trapped animals early so fire crews are not left fishing blindly in narrow pipes, especially when young cats still learning their territory wander near planter edges after rain.

Community volunteers in Stanley say similar calls rise in hot weather when animals seek cooler spaces and slip into utility openings; quick reports to police or the SPCA, plus patience with food rather than forcing the pipe, usually give the safest outcome for a six-week-old kitten with no injuries.
