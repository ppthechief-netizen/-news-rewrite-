11th August 2026 - (Hong Kong) Extreme heat across the city, with several districts topping 39 degrees Celsius and record highs reported, has again left the Labour Department's Work Heat Warning at the lowest yellow tier, prompting public doubt that the system reflects frontline heat stress.

Mr. HUI Chark Shum, Sam, JP, Commissioner for Labour, told RTHK's Millennium programme today that colleagues watching temperature trends during the historic spike saw the warning level fail to rise. "We also have many question marks," he said. On Sunday, 9th August, areas such as Sheung Shui logged more than 39 degrees, yet the warning never escalated to red or black.

Mr Hui said the department refined the trigger mechanism in April, drawing in more Observatory monitoring data and expecting a higher chance of stronger warnings this summer, but outcomes fell short. After media enquiries, officials immediately reviewed the data and are discussing refinements with the Observatory.

The current heat-stress model uses the internationally recognised wet bulb globe temperature (WBGT) index, adapted locally by the Observatory and CU Medicine. Because Hong Kong's humid subtropical climate often stresses workers through moisture and poor ventilation, the weighting for humidity, wind and related factors was raised from the usual 70 per cent to 80 per cent. Mr Hui acknowledged that outdoor radiant heat carries relatively less weight, so workers under direct sun may face risks the index does not capture quickly enough. One review track is to raise sensitivity for outdoor exposure and high ambient heat.

Asked whether an abnormal territory-wide temperature spike could directly activate a higher warning beyond the local heat index, or whether monitoring-station thresholds could be eased for swifter alerts, Mr Hui said both ideas are under study pending expert talks, though they mirror recent public suggestions. Per-district warnings are harder to run consistently, he added, so the focus stays on territory-wide triggers and sensitivity.

On Commercial Radio's On a Clear Day programme, Mr Hui said the WBGT framework still suits poorly ventilated shaded sites such as building lobbies and bus termini, and the department does not plan to discard that base. Instead it is examining "dynamic adjustment": when overall temperatures are unusually high and the Observatory issues extreme-heat guidance, thresholds for higher warning levels tied to ambient heat and radiation would be lowered. That blend of humidity and pure high-temperature risk could make escalation easier; under the proposed approach, the past two days might have justified a red warning. He aims to finish the review before this summer ends.

Under a yellow warning, workers doing "very heavy" tasks such as rebar fixing and bamboo scaffolding must rest 45 minutes after every 15 minutes of work. Lawrence Ng San-wa, president of the Hong Kong Construction Sub-contractors Association, told the same programme that routine sites cannot operate that way. Walking to rest areas consumes time, leaving barely 15 productive minutes an hour, so managers rarely enforce the cycle. When conditions become that severe, he said, stopping work until the warning eases is more realistic for safety.

Mr Ng backed a government review, noting the expert-designed system weighs temperature, humidity and other inputs in ways that feel abstract on site. "The easiest thing to see is the temperature," he said, urging officials to explore simpler, temperature-led indicators frontline staff can follow without waiting solely for government signals.
