11th August 2026 - (Hong Kong) Former Observatory director Lam Chiu-ying has called the Labour Department's decision to keep only a yellow Work Heat Warning on Hong Kong's record-hot day "out of touch", while Under Secretary for Labour and Welfare Mr. HO Kai Ming, JP said the department would study how to adjust.

On 9th August the city hit a record high of 36.9 degrees Celsius, yet no red or black work heat warning was issued. Mr Lam said keeping yellow was not the Observatory's call and that blaming the weather service was unfair.

He argued the current guide underweights outdoor sun exposure and should use black-globe and wet-bulb temperatures. Black-globe readings capture direct sun and radiation from asphalt and walls; wet-bulb readings, with a wetted ventilated sensor, reflect how well sweat can cool the body.

Mr Lam said the Observatory once prepared to release black-globe and wet-bulb figures publicly but a "certain department" objected, fearing labour-sector alarm, and pushed an index that does not fit open-air work.

He said the Work Heat Warning uses the Hong Kong Heat Index without consulting the Observatory, and that the index was built to track sudden hospital admissions for the general public, who mostly stay indoors so sun exposure weighs little.

Under Secretary Ho said the Labour Department keeps talking with units including the Observatory, will research further and see how to adjust, and has heard the public and the former director.

Asked whether a department blocked publishing black-globe and wet-bulb data, he did not answer directly, saying only that the government would try to cut workers' injury risk to a minimum.

Construction and outdoor crews who worked through Sunday's record heat say the gap between street conditions and a day-long yellow alert is exactly why Mr Lam wants radiation and humidity built into the warning ladder.
