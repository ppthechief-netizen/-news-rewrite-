11th August 2026 - (Hong Kong) Workers have ringed parts of Bishop Hill in Sham Shui Po with wire mesh and hoarding as the government prepares a phased clearance of illegal and unsafe structures, fitness gear and clutter built by residents over many years.

The authorities announced on 10th August that unlawful and unsafe structures, facilities and sundries on the hill would be removed in stages, and that religious leaders would be arranged to handle the many idols placed there for worship. At the same time they published a "demolish first, build later" upgrade plan covering seven types of improvement works. Officials said that, mindful of demand for recreation, they would push the seven packages at suitable and safe spots on Woh Chai Shan — the hill's official name — including a new series of fitness facilities and more table-tennis tables at safe positions on the hill and at Tai Hang Tung Playground.

Journalists who visited that afternoon found Lands Department notices posted on the DIY installations ordering an end to unlawful occupation of government land. Some neighbours still climbed the hill to walk or exercise as usual. A resident surnamed Tsui said he was sorry to see the community-built kit go, stressing that the fitness machines and table-tennis tables had been paid for and erected by residents without a dollar of public money. "People made the place nicer, and now the government says it will take it back," he said, asking where locals would play if the gear were removed. He argued that the rear hill sits away from housing and that people seeking simple recreation caused little harm.

Tsui also confirmed seeing large Buddhas, Guanyin statues and figures of the God of Longevity among the idols. He said many households can no longer burn incense or keep shrines at home, so residents carried the figures uphill rather than discard them. Faith, he added, is personal, and offering incense on the hill is ordinary, though the government now says the displays occupy space.

Despite regret over losing the self-built apparatus, Tsui strongly supported the promise of brand-new facilities and extra table-tennis tables once he heard of it. He said he had not known about the plan earlier but called it welcome, describing official replacements as something residents had long hoped for.

Bishop Hill's unofficial gym culture grew as neighbours occupied government land for recreation and worship. The staged clearance aims to cut safety risks from makeshift structures while the later works restore standardised kit. Whether the seven upgrade packages can match the informal amenity residents built remains the practical test once fencing comes down and construction begins.
