10th August 2026 - (Hong Kong) The Labour Department issued another yellow work heat stress warning at 7.00am today as tropical cyclone White Dolphin's peripheral sinking air kept the city extremely hot, shortly after the Observatory raised its Very Hot Weather Warning at 6.45am, telling employers and staff again to cut heat-stroke risk outdoors and in non-air-conditioned workplaces.
Under the department's scale tied to the Observatory heat stress index, yellow covers 30 to below 32, red spans 32 to 34, and black is above 34. Employers must assess heat-stress factors and, so far as reasonably practicable, rearrange hours, erect shade, improve ventilation and cooling, remind staff to drink and rest, and — while a warning is in force — follow the Guide on Preventing Heat Stroke at Work with hourly rest matched to workload and other risk factors for outdoor or non-air-conditioned indoor crews. Workers who develop headache, dizziness, thirst or nausea should move to shade, drink water and alert supervisors at once.
Former Observatory director Lam Chiu-ying criticised the scheme on Facebook, saying a record-breaking bake still only yields yellow and that heat stroke is not treated as an occupational injury, leaving the system almost ornamental. He argues the Observatory index was designed mainly for people active indoors, with black-globe solar radiation weighted too lightly for those in the sun, and that many countries lean more on wet-bulb and black-globe temperatures. Japan, he notes, treats workplace heat illness more readily as an injury once WBGT tops 28C or air temperature exceeds 31C for continuous work beyond an hour or four hours in a day. Even with Sheung Shui hitting 39.8C, he said the local index stayed around 31 — one point short of red — and warned that if today's extremes cannot trigger red, the higher bands may barely appear for years.
