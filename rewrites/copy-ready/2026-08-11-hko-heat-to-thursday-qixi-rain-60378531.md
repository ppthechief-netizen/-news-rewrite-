11th August 2026 - (Hong Kong) The Hong Kong Observatory expects extreme heat to linger until Thursday, after tropical cyclone Bailutang's outer subsiding air helped deliver the hottest day in nearly 143 years of records and left Sheung Shui still baking above 39 degrees Celsius on 10th August.

On 9th August the Observatory's Tsim Sha Tsui headquarters logged 36.9 degrees Celsius, a record since 1884, while the Sheung Shui automatic station reached 39.8 degrees Celsius — the highest reading since such stations were installed in the 1980s. By about 2.50pm on 10th August a Very Hot Weather Warning remained in force, Tsim Sha Tsui had touched 34.9 degrees Celsius, Sheung Shui peaked at 39.3 degrees Celsius, and the Labour Department's yellow Work Heat Warning was still active.

A special weather tip issued at noon on 10th August said Guangdong was widely fine and that Hong Kong would stay extremely hot today and tomorrow, with some districts at 37 degrees Celsius or above, until an upper-air disturbance later in the week brings a few showers and slight relief. Residents were told to watch their health, drink more water, take heat precautions and avoid vigorous outdoor exercise. Dizziness, headache, nausea, shortness of breath or confusion should prompt immediate rest, help and prompt medical care.

The nine-day forecast updated that morning put Tuesday's urban range at 29 to 35 degrees Celsius, relative humidity 50 to 90 per cent, with moderate west to south-west winds, mainly fine weather and extreme daytime heat, though local showers may develop later. District guidance for 2pm tomorrow still shows about 37 degrees Celsius in parts of the urban area and the New Territories, including Wong Tai Sin, Yuen Long and Tai Wo, with Sheung Shui climbing towards 38 degrees Celsius.

A south-west airstream is expected to affect the Guangdong coast over the following day or two, keeping temperatures high with a few showers. Wednesday and Thursday, 12th and 13th August, should see relative humidity of 60 to 90 per cent, moderate south-west winds, sunny periods, local showers and hot days, with urban readings roughly 28 to 34 degrees Celsius on Wednesday and 28 to 33 degrees Celsius on Thursday.

From Friday into the weekend an upper disturbance is forecast to unsettle southern China, increasing showers and easing the peak heat a little. Friday and Saturday, 14th and 15th August, point to urban temperatures of 27 to 32 degrees Celsius, humidity 75 to 95 per cent, moderate south-west winds and generally cloudy skies with a few showers; Friday may bring sunny intervals then local thunderstorms, while Saturday starts with local thunderstorms before brighter spells return.

A broad trough is expected early next week across the northern South China Sea towards seas east of Taiwan. Sunday the 16th looks 27 to 32 degrees Celsius with moderate westerlies, sunny intervals and showers. Monday the 17th may reach 27 to 33 degrees Celsius with light winds, sunny periods, showers and a hot day. Tuesday the 18th and Wednesday the 19th — Qixi, the Chinese Valentine's Day — are pencilled in at 27 to 32 degrees Celsius, humidity 70 to 95 per cent, moderate easterlies, sunny intervals and a few showers.
