11th August 2026 - (Hong Kong) An investigation finds that mainland workers who applied to come to Hong Kong through a Zhuhai middleman called Xin Li Liang include people who paid but never arrived, and others who did arrive only to be placed in work that breached visa conditions and left them as illegal workers, with refund demands ignored.

Xin Li Liang sits among related firms operating in Zhuhai and Hong Kong, including a licensed employment agency in the city. Former shareholders and the legal representative point at each other and deny owing the workers money. Some labourers say they trust Hong Kong as a place ruled by law and want enforcement agencies to act. The Labour Department did not say whether it had received complaints about the agencies involved, only that if a case does not involve a Hong Kong employment agency, jobseekers may need to seek help from mainland authorities.

Workers who dealt with Xin Li Liang — Guangdong Xin Li Liang Human Resources Co Ltd — describe invalid contracts, visas issued after Immigration approval while named employers said they never hired anyone, and placements that turned lawful entry into illegal work. Some went to the Zhuhai office seeking refunds and filmed the encounter. The office signage read Kuai Shou Zhao; when a worker asked when money would be returned, a staff member said within a month and added that if nothing came, "you can report me to the police" and that sitting in custody "two or three times" held no fear.

Mainland company records show Kuai Shou Zhao registered as Zhuhai Kuai Shou Employment Human Resources Co Ltd. Xin Li Liang remains registered and shares the same registered address. Founding shareholder Wu Fan also runs the licensed Hong Kong agency Kuai Shou Zhao and is a shareholder of Hong Kong Labour Group Limited. In an earlier case, Guangzhou salesman Mr Fung paid agency fees for a sales post under a flawed contract; his Hong Kong employer said he first contacted the agent after seeing a "Kuai Shou Zhao imported labour" social-media advert and had never heard of Xin Li Liang.

Wu Fan said he had only been a senior figure at Zhuhai Xin Li Liang, denied that Kuai Shou Zhao was linked to the imported-labour cases, and insisted the Hong Kong agency owed workers nothing and had "absolutely no relationship" with Xin Li Liang. Asked why Zhuhai Kuai Shou Zhao staff answered refund questions, he said he did not know. Records show he ceased to be a Xin Li Liang shareholder in August 2025, replaced by another person surnamed Wu who is now a partner in Wu Fan's Zhuhai Kuai Shou firm. On unpaid refunds, Wu Fan told reporters to contact Xin Li Liang's legal representative, Zhang Jiewei.

Workers also supplied screenshots from social-media clips once posted by Xin Li Liang, showing office-front signs for Hong Kong Labour Group Limited and the Hong Kong Kuai Shou Zhao imported-labour employment agency — the same names Wu Fan uses in Hong Kong. Zhang Jiewei said she was dismissed in early October 2025, claimed the problem cases arose after she left, and said she had not dealt with the employers involved. She also said Xin Li Liang already displayed Kuai Shou Zhao signage at the door when it opened.

Reporters visited the Hong Kong subsidiary of labour firm Zhongfu. Staff said some workers had sought help and that a visit to the Kuai Shou Zhao office in the same building drew the reply that shareholders were in dispute and in court, so the firm "dared not refund". In October 2025 Zhang Jiewei placed a notice in International Business Daily, under China's Ministry of Commerce, saying the company seal and her personal seal had been unlawfully held and used by others and that internal disputes existed.

Mr Fung and other workers affected by the same middleman came to Hong Kong to report the matter to police, saying they believed the city would deliver a proper judgment. At least five also reported in Zhuhai. Police confirmed reports had been received: complainants said they paid a mainland agency for help applying to work in Hong Kong and later found only part of the promised service was delivered. After a preliminary review, police treated the matter as a dispute rather than a criminal case. The Labour Department again gave no figure for complaints about the agencies, stressing it takes alleged breaches by employment agencies seriously, but that complaints about mainland labour brokers with no Hong Kong agency link may need to go to mainland departments.
