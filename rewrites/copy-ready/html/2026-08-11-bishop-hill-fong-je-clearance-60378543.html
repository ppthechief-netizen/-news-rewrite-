11th August 2026 - (Hong Kong) The caretaker of informal leisure space on Bishop Hill in Sham Shui Po says the government is pressing ahead with clearance against neighbours' wishes, even as officials outline a two-stage plan to remove illegal and unsafe structures and later add table tennis tables.

Lee Lan-fong, known as Sister Fong, chairs the Bishop Hill Sports Association. Speaking by phone, she said the recreation area is already cordoned off and that Lands Department staff told her demolition could begin tomorrow or the day after, with the public barred from entering. She has contacted several government departments but sees no clear next step to keep the facilities, arguing officials have brushed aside local feeling. Residents' lowest demand, she said, is that table tennis tables remain.

For years, near Tong Yam Street at the foot of the hill, neighbours built what they treat as a public space with exercise gear and religious statues on government land. Earlier clearance attempts failed to reclaim the site. The government on 10th August announced an enhancement plan for Bishop Hill — formally Wo Chai Shan — in two stages to clear unlawful and unsafe structures, facilities or clutter, with religious leaders invited to help handle religious items and table tennis tables promised later. Stage one covers the hillside rest area from Tong Yam Street up to the summit; stage two cordons the slope from near Berwick Street to the top.

Sister Fong said twenty years of peaceful use are now to be torn out at once and asked what has become of Hong Kong. She recalled more than 3,000 signatures in 2022 opposing demolition of the morning-exercise and fitness kit built by residents, calling the latest decision stubbornly one-track.

Officials say religious leaders will be invited to help deal with statues and other religious items on the slope, while the enhancement plan also contemplates putting table tennis tables back in an authorised form after the clearances. That promise is the one detail Sister Fong has welcomed, even as she disputes the pace and breadth of the demolitions.

Asked how she would keep fighting, she said there was little left to do after fruitless approaches to departments. Citing the city's push for tourism everywhere, she stressed that every leisure item was privately funded and free, and that visitors had come from afar to photograph the spot. If even that is cleared, she asked, what remains to draw people? She grew tearful during the interview and said some neighbours cried when they heard the news. On the promised official table tennis tables she allowed that the plan was "quite good" and matched the minimum residents want.
