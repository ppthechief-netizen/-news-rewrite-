10th August 2026 - (Hong Kong) Hong Kong Trade Development Council chair Professor Frederick Ma Si-hang, GBS, JP cast Hong Kong-Malaysia ties as far deeper than trade figures, pointing to Malaysians who have long put down roots and shone in the city.

The council's flagship overseas promotion Think Business, Think Hong Kong is being staged in Kuala Lumpur after Jakarta in January 2025 and Milan in November. Organisers want Malaysian businesses to see Hong Kong's latest strengths and how the city can open the Chinese mainland, the Guangdong-Hong Kong-Macao Greater Bay Area, ASEAN and other markets.

The programme focuses on finance, innovation and technology, healthcare, professional services and environmental services, with a Hong Kong luncheon meant to deepen business exchanges. Hong Kong is pitched as a bridge for mainland firms entering ASEAN: council surveys show more than 90 per cent of mainland companies plan ASEAN expansion, and more than 80 per cent of those want to use Hong Kong professional services.

Mr Ma said many Malaysian friends are already part of Hong Kong society. He named Cheah Cheng Hye, Yeoh Eng-kiong and Michelle Yeoh as standout Malaysians who have flourished here. Many Hong Kong brands, from banks to hotels, are likewise planted across Malaysia — an "you in me, I in you" bond, he said.

Hong Kong's passion for Malaysian durian, especially Musang King, runs to "obsession", he joked, and the Kuala Lumpur trip falls in peak season, so many business delegates are ready to feast.

Secretary for Financial Services and the Treasury Mr. HUI Ching Yu, Christopher, GBS, JP has been leading financial and accounting regulators on a Malaysia visit. The Securities and Futures Commission and the Securities Commission Malaysia signed a memorandum expanding fund mutual recognition and simplifying dual-listing arrangements, with a simplified dual IPO framework due in September, covering areas including renminbi internationalisation and Islamic finance.

Within a month, Mr Ma said, the council's event continues the Hong Kong push in Malaysia on commerce and professional services while the SAR's Economic and Trade Office has formally opened — dense, linked promotions with clear synergy. He hopes to open more Malaysian opportunities for Hong Kong firms and draw Malaysian companies here.

ASEAN is the world's fifth-largest economy after the US, the Chinese mainland, Japan and Germany, Hong Kong's second-largest trading partner with bilateral trade of US$213.9 billion in 2025, and the mainland's largest trading partner. Amid supply-chain reshuffling, ASEAN is becoming a growth engine for capital, manufacturing and consumption.

Malaysia is Hong Kong's eighth-largest trading partner and third within ASEAN. Bilateral trade reached US$27 billion in 2025, up 2.3 times over a decade, and rose more than 40 per cent year on year in the first half of 2026. Cumulative Hong Kong FDI in Malaysia stood at US$34.8 billion by end-2025. Malaysia's New Industrial Master Plan 2030, which targets 3,000 smart factories, is expected to create large demand for industrial upgrading and digital transformation.
