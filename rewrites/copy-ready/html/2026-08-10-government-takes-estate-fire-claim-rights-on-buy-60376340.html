10th August 2026 - (Hong Kong) When the government buys Wang Fuk Court flats, it will also take over claim rights under the owners' corporation fire policy and shoulder the legal, procedural, quantum and timing risks of recovery, the Housing Bureau says, while residents who bought their own fire cover may keep those claim rights after selling — though two policies on one damaged unit will not pay twice.

At a 19th July special owners' meeting — the first after the blaze — convened by government-appointed manager Hop On Management under Hopewell, Taiping Insurance (Hong Kong) explained a fire policy capped at HK$2 billion and said buyouts would transfer estate-policy claim rights; the insurer must await the independent inquiry report before clarifying liability and opening payouts. Asked how personally bought fire cover would be treated, the insurer only said there would be no double indemnity and did not answer whether those claim rights travel with the title. The Bureau later clarified that corporation-policy claims become the government's once it owns the flat, accepting uncertainty over law, process, amounts and time; personal fire policies stay with the selling owner for claims, with insurers sharing proportionally under contribution rules rather than stacking full payouts. Household contents cover, it added, pays the person who was the insured when the loss occurred, so claim rights generally remain with the original owner after sale — provided notice and filing deadlines in the policy are met, which is why owners should lodge claims promptly. Officials stress the Wang Fuk case is unusually complex and the whole claims path is expected to take a long time; without government ownership it would struggle to intervene in estate-policy recovery at all.
