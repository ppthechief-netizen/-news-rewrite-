10th August 2026 - (Hong Kong) When the government buys Wang Fuk Court flats, it will also take over claim rights under the owners' corporation fire policy and shoulder the legal, procedural, quantum and timing risks of recovery, the Housing Bureau says, while residents who bought their own fire cover may keep those claim rights after selling — though two policies on one damaged unit will not pay twice.

At a 19th July special owners' meeting — the first after the blaze — convened by government-appointed manager Hop On Management under Hopewell, Taiping Insurance (Hong Kong) explained a fire policy capped at HK$2 billion and said buyouts would transfer estate-policy claim rights; the insurer must await the independent inquiry report before clarifying liability and opening payouts. Asked how personally bought fire cover would be treated, the insurer only said there would be no double indemnity and did not answer whether those claim rights travel with the title — leaving owners seeking a clearer official line.

Queried by HK01, a Bureau spokesman said following corporation-policy claims is ordinarily a matter between owners and insurers, but once the government becomes the flat owner it takes on that work — and the uncertainties over law, process, amounts and time. For separately purchased fire cover, a damaged unit with two policies still will not receive double pay; insurers share under contribution rules, and selling owners may keep claim rights on their own policies when the government acquires the title.

Household contents cover, it added, indemnifies the person who was the insured when the loss occurred, so if that owner was still the insured within the policy period, claim rights generally remain with the original owner after sale rather than passing to the new title holder — provided notice and filing deadlines in the policy are met, which is why owners should lodge claims promptly after the accident lest recovery chances or amounts shrink.

Officials stress the Wang Fuk case is unusually complex and the whole claims path is expected to take a long time. The estate fire cover was bought by the corporation for the estate, so recovery sits between estate owners and insurers; without government ownership it would struggle to intervene at all. Under the earlier long-term housing arrangement that proposes buying Wang Fuk titles, consenting sellers hand the follow-up insurance work to the government once it owns the flat — meaning the government absorbs those legal, procedural, quantum and timing risks.

Fire payouts turn on actual damage and repair costs, so even where personal and corporation policies both respond, contribution — not stacked full payouts — applies, while personal fire claim rights can stay with the selling owner and contents rights generally stay with the original insured if notice rules are met. The Bureau's clarification thus draws a bright line: corporation fire claims travel with the buyout; personal fire and contents claims, properly notified, stay with the household that suffered the loss.
