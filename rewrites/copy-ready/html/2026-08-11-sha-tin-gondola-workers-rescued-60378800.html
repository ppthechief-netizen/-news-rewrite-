11th August 2026 - (Hong Kong) Two workers trapped mid-air on a tilted suspended gondola beside a Sha Tin rooftop were brought back to safety this morning after building security at New Town Commercial Building alerted police, with neither man injured.

Police received the call at 11.20am from security staff. The pair had been working on the roof when the gondola they were riding suddenly developed a fault and tilted, leaving them stranded in open air opposite the rooftop edge with no way to climb back unaided. Witnesses at the scene could see the craft hanging beside the building while firefighters prepared their approach.

The Fire Services Department High Angle Rescue Team reached the block and worked from the roof. Officers secured safety harnesses on both workers, then carefully eased them one by one off the listing platform and onto solid rooftop ground. The specialised unit — trained for cable cars, tower cranes, scaffolding and building gondolas — carried out the hand-over methodically while the suspended platform remained fixed in mid-air.

Neither worker was hurt and neither required hospital treatment. The brief timeline from the security report to a double rescue underscored how quickly a routine facade or rooftop maintenance job can become a high-angle incident when a platform tilts and will not recover under its own power.

New Town Commercial Building stands at 10-18 Pak Hok Ting Street, a 12-storey block next to New Town Plaza Phase 1 and the Royal Park Hotel in the heart of Sha Tin's shopping strip. The dense mix of malls, hotels and offices means any suspended-platform drama plays out above busy streets and tourist footfall, even when — as today — the outcome is an uninjured return to the roof rather than a longer aerial evacuation.

Suspended working platforms, known locally as gondolas or hanging boats, are standard kit for exterior cleaning and maintenance on mid-rise commercial towers. When motors, winches or balance fail, occupants can be left canted over empty space with little ability to self-recover — precisely the niche the High Angle Rescue Team was created to address within the fire service's specialist roster.

Today's case followed that pattern without a worse ending: a security alert, a rooftop deployment, harnesses clipped on, two workers walked clear, no ambulance run. Employers and investigators typically examine what caused the tilt once lives are secure; the public record so far stops at the fault, the lean, the rescue and the all-clear on injuries at the Sha Tin commercial block overlooking New Town Plaza.
