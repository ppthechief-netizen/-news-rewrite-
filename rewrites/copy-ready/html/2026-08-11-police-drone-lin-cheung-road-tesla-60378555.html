11th August 2026 - (Hong Kong) Hong Kong police have for the first time used drones to catch traffic offences on Lin Cheung Road, stopping a white Tesla that cut across a solid white line and a dump truck that stayed illegally on the fast lane as officers on the ground closed in with iron barriers.

The force's Traffic Headquarters began a territory-wide traffic-offence thematic operation on 3rd August. From 10th August it launched two-week enforcement drives coded Clearview and Fellow Travellers. West Kowloon Region Traffic brought drones into the operation on the first day of those campaigns.

Remote pilots demonstrated the system at the Nam Cheong Park flyover near Lin Cheung Road and the West Kowloon Highway. The aircraft lifted from an orange helipad marked with an H, climbed to about the height of a ten-storey building, then drifted over the carriageway and hovered to watch traffic while a live picture filled a police screen below.

From that feed officers first logged the Tesla's unlawful lane change on Lin Cheung Road, then a dump truck improperly using the fast lane on the West Kowloon Highway. Ground teams were told at once to intercept both vehicles and lay charges.

Police said the focus is inattentive driving: careless driving, using a mobile phone at the wheel and ignoring road rules. Clearview forms part of that wider push. In the first half of this year Hong Kong recorded 59 fatal traffic accidents, up sharply from 41 in the same period last year — a rise of 44 per cent. Of the vehicles involved in those fatal crashes, 41 were commercial vehicles, or 66 per cent of the total.

Force analysis points to driver inattention as a leading cause of fatal accidents. Clearview therefore targets reckless lane cutting, phone use while driving, lingering on the fast lane and tailgating. Alongside the drones, West Kowloon Traffic also fielded undercover "invisible" police cars dressed to look like ordinary private vehicles, aiming at unsafe driving and commercial-vehicle breaches on Lin Cheung Road and the West Kowloon Highway.

Officers said drones cover a wide area, can be moved flexibly and lift enforcement efficiency, letting them stop offenders on the spot or prosecute later. Traffic Headquarters will keep reviewing blackspots and consider aerial patrol and video enforcement on expressways where needed, while urging drivers to stay alert, obey the rules and not gamble with other people's lives.
