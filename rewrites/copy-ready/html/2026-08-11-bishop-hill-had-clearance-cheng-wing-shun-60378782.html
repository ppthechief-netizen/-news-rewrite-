11th August 2026 - (Hong Kong) Illegal structures and safety worries on Bishop Hill in Sham Shui Po have troubled neighbours for more than a decade, and the Home Affairs Department has now formally launched an improvement plan to clear unlawful and dangerous hillside buildings in two phases while rolling out seven enhancement packages.

The hill, also known as Woh Chai Shan, sits between Shek Kip Mei and Sham Shui Po and draws crowds to its Grade 1 historic Roman-style service reservoir. Over the years residents have put up makeshift facilities on the slopes and ridge, including homemade table-tennis tables, fitness equipment and religious idols. DAB legislator Cheng Wing-shun told RTHK's Millennium programme this morning that he welcomes the plan, saying it can balance safety with public recreation and deliver a win-win outcome.

Cheng said there are more than 50 illegal structures on the hill, among them more than ten self-built table-tennis tables and numerous statues. Some installations sit on slopes where heavy rain can trigger soil erosion, and ageing builds create genuine safety risks alongside unlawful occupation of government land. Authorities will cordon the hill in zones for the work, splitting it in half and sealing each half for about three days. Because many religious figures are involved, officials say appropriate religious ceremonies will be arranged to remove and handle them. The Water Supplies Department's Bishop Hill service reservoir will remain open throughout the clearance.

Bishop Hill lies near Nam Shan Estate, Shek Kip Mei Estate, Pak Tin Estate and Tai Hang Tung Estate, where older public housing estates carry a high proportion of elderly residents. Cheng noted that insufficient district recreation facilities had led neighbours to erect gear themselves. He argued that, while protecting the hillside's natural appearance and preventing soil loss, resetting facilities on site can meet residents' needs for exercise and rest.

He suggested the Sham Shui Po District Office-led approach could serve as a model of "district action governance" for similar hills in old Kowloon districts such as Kwun Tong and Wong Tai Sin, where residents have also built their own facilities. Cross-department coordination and early talks with the community, he said, had removed safety hazards while keeping valued public space. Cheng also urged the Water Supplies Department to use the opportunity to improve walking routes and signage linking Bishop Hill with surrounding neighbourhoods.

The seven planned upgrades include repairing seating, replacing fitness facilities and adding table-tennis tables at suitable spots once unlawful and unsafe items are gone. Officials say the staged clearance and later works aim to cut landslip and structural risks without stripping the hill of the recreation neighbours rely on.
