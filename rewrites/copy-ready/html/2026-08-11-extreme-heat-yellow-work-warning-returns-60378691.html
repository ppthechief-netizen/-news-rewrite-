11th August 2026 - (Hong Kong) The Very Hot Weather Warning remains in force and the Labour Department again issued a yellow Work Heat Warning at 7am today, 11th August, as the Observatory forecast extreme heat with sunny skies along the Guangdong coast.

Today should be mainly fine and extremely hot by day, with urban highs near 35 degrees Celsius and the New Territories two or three degrees hotter in places, which can push some districts to about 37 degrees Celsius or above. Isolated showers may develop later under light west to south-westerly winds.

A south-westerly airstream will keep the coast hot for the next day or two with a few showers. An upper disturbance from Friday, 13th August, into the weekend will make South China more unsettled with more showers. A broad trough is expected to affect the northern South China Sea to seas east of Taiwan early next week. Tropical cyclone White Dolphin will move further inland today and weaken.

The nine-day outlook has sunshine and heat tomorrow, 12th August, and Thursday, 13th August — highs about 34C then 33C with local showers. Friday and Saturday will be mainly cloudy with a few showers and thunderstorms in places, brief sun, and temperatures between 27C and 32C.

Monday, 17th August, brings some sun, a few showers and heat again near 33C; Tuesday and Wednesday, 18th and 19th August, have brief sun, a few showers and highs near 32C as the trough begins to reshape regional winds.

Outdoor workers should watch the yellow Work Heat Warning rest rules while urban and New Territories temperatures stay extreme through midweek, and residents are advised to drink water regularly, avoid prolonged exposure in the midday sun, and check on elderly neighbours during the hottest hours.

Schools and construction sites often adjust outdoor drills and works when successive Very Hot Weather Warnings coincide with Work Heat Warnings, and the Observatory will update the nine-day sequence if White Dolphin’s inland decay or the weekend upper disturbance alters rainfall timing across the Pearl River estuary.
