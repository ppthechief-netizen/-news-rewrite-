11th August 2026 - (Hong Kong) A District Court master has refused a Bel-Air neighbour's application to introduce an expert report that would have used a tapping machine and loudspeakers to recreate alleged piano noise from the home of liver-cancer specialist Dr Ronnie Poon Tung-ping.

The plaintiff, On Ning, sued five defendants: Dr Poon, his wife Dr Roberta Pang Wen-chi, their two daughters — including Clarisse Poon, who was linked last year to the MediSafe competition dispute — and the occupants of two units in a block at 28 Bel-Air Road. The writ (DCCJ 7351/2024) seeks an injunction barring the defendants from making, causing or allowing noise and music, including but not limited to piano sound, plus damages for alleged breaches of the deed of mutual covenant and related costs.

Counsel for the plaintiff appeared before Master Melinda Chiang Man-ching and sought leave to rely on expert evidence measuring noise in decibels with a tapping machine and other tools. The neighbour alleges evening piano playing causes a nuisance and wants expert advice on suitable soundproofing. The proposed method would generate standard sounds through a tapping machine and speakers to gauge noise levels and the acoustic grade of the flats.

The defence argued that volumes differ sharply between pieces — for example a children's rhyme such as "Old MacDonald" versus Beethoven — so surrogate noise cannot stand in for real piano performance. The court heard that the Poon household refuses to let others use its piano. Counsel also stressed that even if a defendant played the instrument live, different repertoire, touch and playing times would make it hard to reproduce past volumes exactly.

Master Chiang questioned whether the plaintiff's method could match piano loudness and, after hearing both sides, held that the matters the expert report would address — the defendants' soundproofing and the method of measuring decibels — were not key issues in the case. She refused leave to adduce the expert report and made no order as to costs.

The ruling leaves the civil nuisance claim to proceed without that laboratory-style reconstruction of the contested music. Neighbour noise disputes in private estates often turn on deed covenants, timing and the lived experience of sound rather than engineered proxies, and the master's decision keeps the focus on those core questions rather than experimental measurements.
