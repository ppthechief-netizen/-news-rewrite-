11th August 2026 - (Hong Kong) A retired teacher lost more than HK$3.6 million in life savings after joining a bogus stock-investment sharing group on WhatsApp, one case among more than 100 investment scams reported to police in the past week with combined losses exceeding HK$150 million.

The force's CyberDefender initiative highlighted the teacher's ordeal in a Facebook post, warning that scammers pose as market experts and recruit accomplices to pose as profitable students. The victim thought group members were "all making money" when in reality, police said, "every one of them was a fraudster".

The teacher first spotted a fake investment advertisement on Instagram and clicked through to a WhatsApp group billed as a stock-investment sharing circle. A self-proclaimed "stock investment expert" promptly urged the victim to download a counterfeit investment platform app and open an account there.

At first the retiree was sceptical, but multiple supposed "successful student" members began posting screenshots of handsome returns, and the caution faded. Over six weeks the victim followed instructions from the sham platform's customer service, making more than ten transfers to several different personal bank accounts — a hallmark police repeatedly flag as a scam rather than a regulated brokerage.

On screen the account showed share prices nearly doubling, and the teacher tried to sell the entire holding. Withdrawals failed. The fake customer service then demanded further transfers as a "margin" deposit before any payout, and only then did the victim grasp the trap. Total losses exceeded HK$3.6 million.

CyberDefender urged the public to treat any pitch promising steady profits, guaranteed high returns or transfers into personal accounts as almost certainly fraudulent, because legitimate investing always carries risk. Officers listed three hard rules: do not trust adverts claiming "high returns with zero risk"; do not download investment apps from unknown sources; and do not send money to unexplained personal bank accounts.

Anyone weighing an online opportunity can check phone numbers, web addresses and payee accounts through Scameter on the CyberDefender website or the Scameter+ mobile app before transferring funds. The force also promotes the upgraded Scameter+ app with artificial-intelligence features as a front-line check against bogus platforms.

The week's tally — triple-digit cases and nine-figure losses — shows how quickly a polished social-media funnel can drain a retirement nest egg in just six weeks, turning cautious scepticism into repeated bank transfers once fake classmates start boasting of gains.
