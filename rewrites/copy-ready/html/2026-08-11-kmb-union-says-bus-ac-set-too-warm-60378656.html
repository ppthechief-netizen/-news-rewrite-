11th August 2026 - (Hong Kong) The KMB Staff Rights Union has told the Labour Department that bus cabin air-conditioning set at 25 degrees Celsius is too warm and weak on airflow, risking drivers' health and road safety after Hong Kong's hottest day on record.

The Observatory's Tsim Sha Tsui headquarters hit 36.9 degrees Celsius on 9th August, the hottest day since records began in 1884, while Sheung Shui reached 39.8 degrees, a high for automatic weather stations since the 1980s.

The union said frontline captains work long hours on the road in such heat, so whether cabin cooling is enough directly affects health and safety. It has already met West Kowloon labour relations officers about cabin temperature and airflow and asked whether the setup is an occupational safety issue.

Facing the record extreme heat, the union called cabin cooling no small matter but a citywide welfare issue for bus captains. It will keep pressing and may escalate to the Labour Department, Transport Department and other bureaux depending on the reply.

KMB said it sets cabin temperatures by season, weather and feedback from passengers and drivers. Given recent extreme heat and record highs, it has begun lowering temperature settings across the whole fleet.

Because the change covers every bus, finishing the work will take time, but the company said it would complete it as soon as possible. It will keep checking air-conditioning systems and monitoring cabin temperatures to keep journeys comfortable.

Drivers stuck in stop-start traffic with weak cabin cooling can face fatigue that the union links directly to steering safety, which is why it wants the Labour Department to treat the 25-degree setting as an occupational health file rather than a comfort preference.

Passengers may notice cooler air only gradually as each bus is retuned, the company warned, because a fleet-wide change cannot finish overnight.
