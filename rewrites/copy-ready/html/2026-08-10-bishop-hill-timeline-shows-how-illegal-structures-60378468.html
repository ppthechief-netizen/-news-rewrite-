10th August 2026 - (Hong Kong) Bishop Hill above Berwick Street in Shek Kip Mei — officially Woh Chai Shan — has for years mixed dawn exercise, cut fences and expanding unauthorised builds on government land; today the government opened a two-phase clear-up of unlawful and unsafe structures, prompting a rewind through two decades of how informal gyms, then shrines, outpaced enforcement.

Before the Romanesque summit service reservoir was recognised, neighbours climbed a "sky ladder" they built themselves. Sars survivor So Chi-keung has said that around 2007–08 he found the fenced reservoir plateau, cut wire and added fitness gear, stone tables, a rain shelter and a swing so neighbours could exercise, creating a gathering spot. By 2009 Sham Shui Po's district facilities committee heard Water Supplies say it would cooperate if another department sought recreation use and took on management without harming waterworks, while Leisure and Cultural Services said it would gladly manage later if users followed LCSD rules. In 2016, still before the heritage find, WSD moved to remove two large trees planters had grown; residents objected and councillors called the department bureaucratic, yet WSD insisted the hill was not open to the public and later cited cracks, roots piercing the structure and tree load making public entry unsuitable.

Late 2020 neighbours about to lose the reservoir to demolition revealed the century-old Romanesque works; WSD paused, and the reservoir was graded Grade 1. Reporters then already saw canvas-and-bamboo mahjong shelters and steel frames for fitness and table tennis on nearby paths with no clearance in sight. After heritage tours began, the Sham Shui Po District Management Committee in May 2022 posted notices at seven spots demanding illegal items go by mid-June, drawing more than 3,000 signatures including Mr So's to keep facilities. That June ADPL councillors Tam Tak-ching and Li Ting-fung sought a pause and a mini-park plan; DAB Kowloon West lawmaker and Bishop Hill Concern Group convenor Vincent Cheng Wing-shun urged lawful recreation to replace demolition-without-substitute. Then district officer Paul Wong Yan-yin said seats and a pebble path near Berwick Street were planned as facilities, not compensation, and morning-walk path works would continue after illegal structures went — yet years on, builds multiplied instead. Late last year scaffolding for a self-styled "Mao Zedong cultural hall" by Bishop Hill Sports Association chair Lee Lan-fong appeared at the Tong Yin Street hillside rest area; Lands cleared it to bare ground, only for a Hong Kong Mao Zedong Cultural Hall and large statue to return, prompting fresh clearance orders with a 9th February statue deadline. Asked why the 2022 order stalled, Lands said strong resident opposition led the district management committee to defer clearing older items, while post-June 2022 new occupations still face control — 33 patrols and repeated actions in three months. Mr Cheng has called a "red line" against new builds a balance and still wants lawful table-tennis and sports gear once land is recovered.
