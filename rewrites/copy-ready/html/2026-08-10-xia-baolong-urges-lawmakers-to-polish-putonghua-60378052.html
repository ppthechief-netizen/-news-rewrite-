10th August 2026 - (Hong Kong) Nearly half of the 88 legislators who made a historic study trip to Beijing last month got to speak in a roughly five-hour closed session with Hong Kong and Macao Affairs Office director Xia Baolong — and political chatter says their Putonghua was so "ordinary", and in places barely twisted Cantonese, that he smilingly urged them to study harder rather than expect him to learn Cantonese in the capital.

The delegation was in Beijing from 19th to 25th of last month at the Chinese Academy of Governance on the 15th Five-Year Plan and Xi Jinping Thought, with enterprise and site visits; the day before departure they met Mr Xia not at the West City office building opened last year but in the Great Hall of the People's Hong Kong Hall, underscoring how seriously the centre treated the course.

He listened first as heavyweights including Legislative Council president Starry Lee Wai-king and House Committee chairman Chan Chun-ying spoke in turn, then opened a hands-up scramble so unscheduled members could air course impressions, sector issues and the Hong Kong economy — a free-for-all that brought many less practised speakers to the microphone.

Whether for lack of daily practice or not, many speeches were said to be rather "ordinary" Putonghua, with some sounding like Cantonese read slightly askew. Mr Xia could not resist encouraging the group to improve, joking that once they had come up to Beijing there was no reason he should instead have to polish his Cantonese. Lawmakers insist the nudge carried no scolding — more a joke-laced reminder — though one joked that some colleagues' "Hong Kong-style Putonghua", while short of Louis Koo levels, would be opaque to mainland officials who are not Hong Kong-bred, leaving Guangxi-born deputy director Nong Rong, who understands Cantonese, as the likely interpreter of last resort among officials in the room.

Several had been told to prepare about five minutes and spoke from scripts rather than improvising on the spot, prompting the dig that with a script in hand they should at least have drilled every character's pronunciation before leaving, lest they become a laughing stock. Another said patriots who cleared every vetting gate to enter LegCo have no excuse for unintelligible Mandarin when "patriots administering Hong Kong" means constant mainland contact and they should raise their own standards without waiting to be teased.

It is not Mr Xia's first linguistic scrape: during his third Hong Kong inspection last year, Liberal Party chairman Tommy Cheung Yu-yan spoke entirely in Cantonese until Chief Executive John Lee Ka-chiu smoothed in Putonghua that a text had been filed beforehand and thanked Mr Xia for his forbearance — drawing no reply. Insiders say this month's remark is unrelated goodwill after time has passed, not delayed score-settling with a hidden meaning linking the two episodes.

Yet as Hong Kong deepens into national development and Putonghua's importance grows by the day, his repeated cue at the symposium to strengthen study is hard to miss — and lawmakers, the column suggests, might as well seize the moment to improve rather than treat the smile as only a soft joke.
