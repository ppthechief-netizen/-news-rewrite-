10th August 2026 - (Hong Kong) Nearly half of the 88 legislators who made a historic study trip to Beijing last month got to speak in a roughly five-hour closed session with Hong Kong and Macao Affairs Office director Xia Baolong — and political chatter says their Putonghua was so "ordinary", and in places barely twisted Cantonese, that he smilingly urged them to study harder rather than expect him to learn Cantonese in the capital.

The delegation was in Beijing from 19th to 25th of last month at the Chinese Academy of Governance on the 15th Five-Year Plan and Xi Jinping Thought, with enterprise and site visits; the day before departure they met Mr Xia not at the West City office building opened last year but in the Great Hall of the People's Hong Kong Hall, underscoring the grade of the trip. He listened first as heavyweights including Legislative Council president Starry Lee Wai-king and House Committee chairman Chan Chun-ying spoke, then opened a hands-up scramble so unscheduled members could air course impressions, sector issues and the Hong Kong economy. Lawmakers insist his Mandarin nudge carried no scolding — more a joke-laced reminder — though one joked that some colleagues' "Hong Kong-style Putonghua", while short of Louis Koo levels, would be opaque to mainland officials who are not Hong Kong-bred, leaving Guangxi-born deputy director Nong Rong, who understands Cantonese, as the likely interpreter of last resort. Several had been told to prepare about five minutes and spoke from scripts, prompting the dig that they should at least have drilled every character's pronunciation before leaving. Another said patriots who cleared every vetting gate have no excuse for unintelligible Mandarin when "patriots administering Hong Kong" means constant mainland contact.

It is not Mr Xia's first linguistic scrape: during his third Hong Kong inspection last year, Liberal Party chairman Tommy Cheung Yu-yan spoke entirely in Cantonese until Chief Executive John Lee Ka-chiu smoothed in Putonghua that a text had been filed beforehand and thanked Mr Xia for his forbearance — drawing no reply. Insiders say this month's remark is unrelated goodwill, not delayed score-settling, yet as Hong Kong deepens into national development the cue to raise Putonghua standards is hard to miss.
