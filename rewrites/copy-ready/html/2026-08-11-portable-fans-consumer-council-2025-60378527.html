11th August 2026 - (Hong Kong) As the Hong Kong Observatory issued a Very Hot Weather Warning and the Labour Department posted a yellow working heat stress warning on the morning of 10th August — the day after a record heat spell — portable rechargeable fans again became a street-level cooling choice for people outdoors.

The Consumer Council tested eighteen portable charging fans for safety, airflow, charging efficiency, battery life, power thrift, quietness and ease of use. The sample covered sixteen handheld models and two neck-hang or ring designs priced from fifty-eight dollars to two hundred and thirty-nine dollars.

Among heavier handheld units, airflow ranged from 1.25 to 2.98 cubic metres a minute. Shenzhen brand Gongtian led at 2.98 cubic metres a minute — about 2.3 times the output of last-placed XPower. JisuLife reached 2.25 cubic metres a minute. In the lighter handheld group, Francfranc topped the list at 2.34 cubic metres a minute, with BRUNO close behind at 2.23.

Run at the highest speed setting with any "cold compress" function switched off, JisuLife and MUJI lasted more than three hours before the battery ran out — the strongest endurance among the handheld samples tested.

On energy use, heavier handheld fans needed between 5.42 and 14.63 watt-hours to move one hundred cubic metres of air, with JisuLife the thriftiest at 5.42 watt-hours. Lighter handheld models used between 2.57 and 12.01 watt-hours for the same volume. Francfranc, BRUNO, HÓME CÓORDY, MUJI, RHYTHM and Proluxury sat in a relatively efficient band of 2.57 to 5.96 watt-hours.

Price for performance favoured MUJI: at fifty-eight dollars it earned an overall 4.5 score. JisuLife and Gongtian also scored 4.5 but cost three hundred and thirty-nine dollars and one hundred and eighty dollars respectively — a reminder that strong airflow and battery life need not match the cheapest ticket, yet the lowest-priced MUJI still matched the top overall mark.

Shoppers facing street heat often weigh airflow against battery life and noise. The council's 2025 round put those trade-offs in numbers across eighteen models, giving buyers a clearer map of which handheld and neck-style fans move the most air, last longest at full blast and waste the least power per cubic metre delivered.
