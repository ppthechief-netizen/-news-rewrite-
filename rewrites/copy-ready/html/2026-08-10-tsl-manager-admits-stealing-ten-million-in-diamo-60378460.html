10th August 2026 - (Hong Kong) A procurement manager who worked more than 30 years at Tse Sui Luen Jewellery revealed years of diamond theft when the company made him redundant. Today (10th August) at the High Court he admitted three counts of theft (HCCC109/2026). The case involves diamonds worth more than HK$10 million. After arrest he said he had sold the stolen stones. In mitigation he blamed family pressure that drove a gambling addiction; his Hong Kong Jockey Club account showed average monthly bets of about HK$300,000. Mr Justice Andrew Chan Hing-wai held that he confessed only because he feared the matter would surface, so it was not a true surrender, adjourned sentence to Friday (14th August) and remanded him in custody.

Mui Ka-fai, 63, admitted three theft counts covering the period from 24th April 2023 to 22nd February 2024, involving a total of 2,557.35 carats of diamonds belonging to ISKKON Gems, R.A. Gem Centre Ltd and KGK Jewellery (HK) Ltd.

The facts state that from 1988 he was TSL's procurement manager with sole charge of buying melee diamonds below 0.08 carat from various suppliers. Under TSL rules every diamond purchase had to be declared in the computer system, with consignment records prepared to log quantities. By mutual understanding, diamonds supplied were for inspection and checking only and had to be returned on the supplier's demand. He also had to arrange delivery of diamonds for mainland gem-testing. In 2024, when he learned he was being made redundant, he seemed unusually busy and unsettled. His procurement supervisor noticed and arranged a meeting about his future. At that meeting he confessed that between 2017 and 2023, taking goods in the company's name from suppliers, he had stolen diamonds worth about HK$10 million in all. He kept US-dollar proceeds from reselling the stones at home, then banked the theft proceeds into his Jockey Club account for betting. Whenever a supplier pressed for return of diamonds lent on consignment, he used cash to buy replacement stones from other suppliers to hand back.

Once the company knew, it reported the matter to police. Under caution he said greed led him to misappropriate the diamonds for gambling. Inquiries showed frequent betting on his Jockey Club account at the material time, averaging as much as HK$300,000 a month. When officers searched his home they seized several bags of melee diamonds worth about HK$2 million, plus consignment records he had signed and stamped for TSL. TSL confirmed that 31 consignment notes he handled were never registered in the company system, covering diamonds worth about HK$11.15 million. The suppliers said they believed he was dealing for Tse Sui Luen because TSL was a long-term customer, so they had not chased returns or payment until the fraud came to light. Defence counsel pleaded that family pressure left him addicted to gambling, that he is divorced, deeply regrets the offences, and hoped the court would weigh his voluntary confession to the company. Mr Justice Andrew Chan Hing-wai rejected that characterisation, saying he spoke up only after dismissal when he feared exposure, refused to treat it as a surrender, remanded him and fixed sentence for Friday 14th August.
