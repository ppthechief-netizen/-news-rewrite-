11th August 2026 - (Hong Kong) Queen Mary Hospital on 10th August introduced an adjustable urethral pressure technique for severe incontinence, saying some patients have regained freedom after years of leakage that blocked ordinary exercise.

The device is placed at the perineum to gently support urethral muscle, with a hidden implant under the skin of the scrotum or pubic area linked to a fluid reservoir. Doctors can later inject saline through a subcutaneous port in clinic, without further open surgery, to match how badly the patient leaks.

Unlike artificial urinary sphincters and male urethral slings, the hospital said the new device suits any degree of post-prostatectomy incontinence. With no complex mechanical pump, clinical series put the chance of needing replacement or removal at about 5.75 per cent — unlike older sphincters that often need swapping every five to ten years — and unlike slings that cannot be retuned and may stiffen as tissue fibroses.

Non-surgical care remains available. Pelvic-floor training with Kegel exercises helps some patients avoid an operation. The hospital also has an electromagnetic therapy chair that stimulates motor nerves to strengthen pelvic muscles when patients cannot perform the exercises correctly.

Dr. Ada Tsui-Lin Ng, consultant and head of urology at Queen Mary Hospital, said that once a patient is assessed as needing surgery, no extra charge is levied beyond ordinary public clinic fees.

The hospital welcomes other public hospitals to refer suitable cases and hopes to keep improving care after radical prostate cancer surgery and to develop still less invasive options.

For men who have lived with severe leakage for years, the ability to return to gym work and sport is presented as the practical test of whether the adjustable support delivers more than continence scores alone.

Patients who once planned every outing around toilets and absorbent pads may find the outpatient fine-tuning of saline volume as important as the initial implant, because continence needs can change as they resume activity.
