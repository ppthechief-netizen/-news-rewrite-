10th August 2026 - (Hong Kong) With Chief Executive John Lee Ka-chiu preparing his fifth policy address — public consultation opened on 29th June alongside views on Hong Kong's first five-year plan — parties and cross-party blocs have tabled a thick wishlist spanning consumption vouchers, housing sales, labour rules, family subsidies, bridge tolls, aviation, space industry offices and elderly health care.

DAB vice-chairman Chan Hok-fung again wants the Tenants Purchase Scheme revived for 20- to 30-year-old flats priced around HK$1 million each; he says more than 80 per cent of residents in his Hong Kong Island West constituency would buy, boosting reserves by over HK$10 billion with profits lasting more than 20 years. The FTU seeks a Building Maintenance Authority to run tendering, assessment and supervision on a pilot of 39 TPS estates to cut bid-rigging, plus a fund for families after workplace sudden deaths, citing global research linking more than 840,000 annual deaths to work-related disease, long hours and stress. Liberals urge electronic cash vouchers for next year's 30th anniversary of the handover — HK$100 off every HK$300 — plus domestic-helper expense deductions and progressive child allowances.

Business and Professionals Alliance figures who met Mr Lee with about 130 proposals across nine areas pressed lower Hong Kong-Zhuhai-Macao Bridge tolls: vice-chairman Lau Ip-keung noted private cars can pay more than RMB300 for a round trip except on free days, and wants monthly and annual passes, simpler applications and a longer-term look at extending "Guangdong cars south" toward northern mainland plates. Party leader Ng Wing-ka wants more flight routes and frequencies as airline capacity sits above 90 per cent of pre-pandemic levels to serve the Greater Bay Area and Belt and Road flows. New People's Party chairwoman Regina Ip Lau Suk-yee still wants a space-economy co-ordination office filled by open recruitment within six months, plus a fourth harbour tunnel from Tseung Kwan O to Hong Kong Island to ease three near-saturated crossings.

The C15+ group wants public housing's rent-to-sale mix shifted from 6:4 toward 5:5 with a Northern Metropolis trial, 95 per cent mortgages for eligible young buyers there, and a means-test-free HK$2,500 student grant next financial year. Federation of Hong Kong and Kowloon Labour Unions chairman Lam Chun-sing floated stronger re-employment subsidies for over-60s — up to HK$15,000 after six months and another HK$15,000 after twelve, mirroring the middle-aged employment scheme — while Chow Siok-chung urged second-tier imported-labour triggers by vacancy, wage and unemployment data, a HK$100-per-worker levy on foreign-labour employers and a catering transition fund paid from that levy.

G19 backs rent-to-buy trials on subsidised Northern Metropolis homes, rolling 12-month average income tests for public tenants, land and tax sweeteners and "land for industry" or Northern Metropolis bonds including offshore renminbi tech debt for key firms, Cross-boundary Payment Connect limits up to HK$50,000 a day, and — from convenor Kenneth Fok Kai-kong — rebuilding Victoria Park's tennis courts into a 10,000-seat all-weather arena. Election Committee member Fan Kai-kit wants newborn bonuses made permanent and raised to HK$30,000; New Territories associations' Tam Chun-kwok seeks a restored HK$2,500 student grant and a carefully priced TPS revival with repair funds and sale bans; Koon Ho-ming wants higher childcare centre parent subsidies, hours to 8.00pm, weekend and emergency care, progressive child allowances and grandparent carer relief, plus elderly health vouchers lifted toward HK$3,000 a year and HK$10,000 caps usable for private physiotherapy and mental health, and MPF-linked medical savings for check-ups and vaccines. The FTU also wants vouchers usable in more Fujian and Hainan clinics and Macau-style subsidies for mainland medical insurance; Maggie Chan Man-ki proposes lifting the Small Claims Tribunal ceiling from HK$75,000 to HK$100,000, a helper employment tax deduction and a bidding law against collusion and bribery.
