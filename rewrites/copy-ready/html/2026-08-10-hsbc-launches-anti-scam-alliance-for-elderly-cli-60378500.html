10th August 2026 - (Hong Kong) With 22 per cent of first-half bank-recorded scam cases by amount involving elderly victims, HSBC Hong Kong has launched a community education programme, the Anti-scam Alliance, to raise older clients' fraud awareness through scenario case studies that help them spot common scam patterns.

The bank is working with Caritas Hong Kong and local social enterprise Eldpathy to compile and share a series of anti-scam fables aimed at sharpening silver-hair fraud awareness and understanding of dementia. The first event has already been held and drew 100 elderly participants.

Bank figures show that, measured by sum, more than one-fifth — 22 per cent — of scam cases recorded by the bank in the first half of this year involved older people. The Alliance is one strand of HSBC's wider push to improve elderly- and dementia-friendly banking. Volunteers use situational cases to guide seniors in recognising familiar fraud scripts, and the project is expected to reach more than 500 older people.

Dementia is also a focus of the bank's elderly-service upgrades; Alliance sessions include dementia sharing that covers common symptoms and carers' needs. Frankie Yan Man-fai, HSBC Hong Kong's head of retail and wealth management retail and wealth product distribution, said the bank serves more than 1.8 million elderly clients and that protecting their assets for later life is central to its "community banking" offer.

Beyond continually improving elderly banking services, he said, HSBC has long promoted anti-scam education, and through the Alliance it wants fraud-prevention tips to feel grounded and memorable so older people share the fables with family and friends and apply what they learn day to day.

The bank added that, with the Alliance's launch, further related measures will be announced later this year, including upgrades to branch facilities and fee waivers aligned with the Elderly-friendly Banking Services Guidelines. It already runs a suite of elderly- and dementia-friendly steps: barrier-free access across all branches, digital ambassadors and community care counters; fuller front-line training on elderly- and dementia-friendly service to lift standards across the team; and, for older clients, the BBAI autonomous personal account and the Money Safe deposit account to prepare for the future and lock funds, cutting scam risk.
