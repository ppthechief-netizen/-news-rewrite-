11th August 2026 - (Hong Kong) The Water Supplies Department said this morning that a reported leak on Hoi Kwai Road in Tsuen Wan involves an 800 millimetre diameter salt-water supply pipe, and that repair teams are struggling with technical difficulties that will affect flushing water in parts of Tsuen Wan and Kwai Chung.

Engineers have been scheduling supplies to keep the affected area as small as possible, the department said on its official social media channel. Because maintenance work requires isolating the main, some buildings in the two districts will see interrupted salt-water service used for toilet flushing while crews work on the fault.

The department noted that most blocks store flushing water in internal tanks, which should cushion the outage so most users notice little disruption despite the leak. Teams are pressing ahead with repairs and aim to finish the pipe work and restore flushing supplies by noon today, though engineers cautioned that the technical obstacles on the 800 millimetre main could still shift that timetable if isolation or welding work proves slower than planned.

Officials said they are in close contact with the Tsuen Wan and Kwai Tsing District Office, the Housing Department, district councillors and relevant management offices as the job proceeds. They apologised for the inconvenience caused to residents and urged estates to monitor tank levels while the main remains out of service, adding that management companies should warn households if stored flushing water runs low before supplies return.

Salt-water mains feed a large share of Hong Kong's flushing network, and leaks on major diameters can ripple across several housing estates before isolation valves take effect. The Hoi Kwai Road fault sits on a trunk line serving dense Tsuen Wan and Kwai Chung neighbourhoods, which explains why engineers have prioritised supply scheduling even as they tackle the technical obstacles on site. Past bursts on comparable mains have sometimes required lane closures and extended night shifts to weld replacement sections without cutting fresh-water supplies.
