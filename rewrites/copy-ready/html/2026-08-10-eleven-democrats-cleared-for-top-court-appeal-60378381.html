10th August 2026 - (Hong Kong) The Court of Appeal today issued certificates clearing eleven defendants convicted in the 47 democrats subversion case to seek a final appeal at the Court of Final Appeal, holding that their National Security Law arguments raise questions of great general or public importance after the same court dismissed conviction and sentence appeals in February (CACC 253/2024).

The eleven — Gordon Ng, Tat Cheng Tat-hung, Clarisse Yeung Suet-ying, Kalvin Ho Kai-ming, Helena Wong Pik-wan, Gwyneth Ho Kwai-lam, Raymond Chan Chi-chuen, Owen Chow Ka-shing, Lam Cheuk-ting, Leung Kwok-hung and Winnie Yu Wai-ming — attended in good spirits and waved to the public gallery; the certificate application was disposed of on paper without the three justices who heard it — Chief Judge of the High Court Mr Justice Jeremy Poon Shiu-chor, Mr Justice Derek Pang Wai-cheong and Mr Justice William Tam Yiu-ho — reading the result in open court. Prosecutors had opposed the leave certificate; the defence cluster includes how to read "other unlawful means" in National Security Law Article 22, whether triggering Basic Law Articles 50 to 52 can amount to such means, whether legislators' constitutional roles and the alleged course of conduct are prohibited acts under Article 22(3), whether the non-intervention principle applies, and whether intent to trigger the Basic Law mechanism shows specific subversive intent. The court listed related issues on whether "unlawful means" is limited to force or threats or to criminal acts, whether the alleged plot and series of acts meet the offence elements, and how legislative budget scrutiny fits the charge. Certificates are the usual gateway to the top court even where leave can also be sought there directly — and these eleven had already filed CFA leave applications. Of the original 47 charged with conspiracy to subvert state power, two — Lee Yue-shun and Lawrence Lau Wai-chung — were acquitted after trial, leaving 45 convicted with terms from four years and two months to ten years; some who pleaded guilty have already finished their sentences. Of twelve who appealed the trial, only Wong Chi-yuet confined her challenge to sentence and has not moved to the CFA; the Department of Justice has not further appealed Lau's acquittal after that bid also failed in the Court of Appeal.
