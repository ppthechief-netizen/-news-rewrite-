10th August 2026 - (Hong Kong) In the 47 democrats subversion case, twelve convicted defendants appealed against conviction and sentence; the Court of Appeal dismissed those appeals in February this year (CACC 253/2024). Eleven of the twelve have now sought a final appeal and applied to the Court of Appeal for a certificate of leave to appeal to the Court of Final Appeal. The Court of Appeal today (10th August) handed down reasons granting the certificates.

The eleven are Gordon Ng, Tat Cheng Tat-hung, Clarisse Yeung Suet-ying, Kalvin Ho Kai-ming, Helena Wong Pik-wan, Gwyneth Ho Kwai-lam, Raymond Chan Chi-chuen, Owen Chow Ka-shing, Lam Cheuk-ting, Leung Kwok-hung and Winnie Yu Wai-ming. All eleven attended court in good spirits and waved to people in the public gallery. The certificate application was not called as a hearing: the three judges who dealt with it — Chief Judge of the High Court Mr Justice Jeremy Poon Shiu-chor, Mr Justice Derek Pang Wai-cheong and Mr Justice William Tam Yiu-ho — did not come into court to read the outcome; the ruling was issued in written reasons.

The reasons record that the eleven argued their points of law are of great general or public importance, while the Department of Justice opposed the applications. The main disputes they raised include how to interpret "other unlawful means" in Article 22 of the National Security Law; whether triggering the mechanism in Basic Law Articles 50 to 52 amounts to such "other unlawful means"; whether legislators' constitutional duties, powers and functions, and their alleged conduct, are prohibited acts under Article 22; whether the non-intervention principle applies in this case; and whether an intention to trigger the Basic Law Articles 50 to 52 mechanism shows a specific subversive intent.

The Court of Appeal held that the issues engage the elements of the Article 22 offence — both the actus reus and the mental element — as well as how legislators discharge their duties, especially scrutiny of the Budget and the Basic Law Articles 50 to 52 mechanism. Because the legal points are of wide importance, the court said they should go to the Court of Final Appeal. Related questions listed for the top court include whether "other unlawful means" is limited to the use or threat of force, or only to criminal acts; whether the alleged "plot" amounts to unlawful means under Article 22; whether the alleged "series of acts" is prohibited conduct under Article 22(3); whether the non-intervention principle applies; and whether a defendant who intends, for the purposes of that plot and series of acts, to trigger Basic Law Articles 50 to 52 and their consequences harbours the specific subversive intent required.

Under the ordinary appeal path, a party seeking to go to the Court of Final Appeal must first apply to the Court of Appeal for a leave certificate; even if refused, leave can still be sought from the Court of Final Appeal itself. These eleven had already filed CFA leave applications. The original case involved 47 defendants, each charged with one count of conspiracy to subvert state power. Two — Lee Yue-shun and Lawrence Lau Wai-chung — were acquitted after trial, leaving 45 convicted and sentenced to terms ranging from four years and two months to ten years. Some who pleaded guilty have already finished their sentences and been released. Of the twelve who appealed the trial outcome, Wong Chi-yuet challenged only her sentence and has not so far appealed to the Court of Final Appeal; the Department of Justice has not further appealed Lau's acquittal after the Court of Appeal also dismissed that prosecution appeal.
