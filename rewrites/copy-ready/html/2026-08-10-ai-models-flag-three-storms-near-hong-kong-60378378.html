10th August 2026 - (Hong Kong) After tropical cyclones Hongxia, Whale and White Dolphin, three AI weather models are already sketching another South China Sea bout next week — though the Hong Kong Observatory has yet to issue any matching forecast.

FengWu, FuXi and Pangu all point to fresh tropical cyclone activity affecting the South China Sea. Europe's ECMWF model likewise sees a system next weekend, but with intensity and track details that diverge from the AI runs. Forecasters stress it remains early: many factors can still reshape the outlook, so the charts are reference only.

FengWu's run is the most theatrical. It forms a cyclone between 17th and 18th August that slips inside Hong Kong's 800-kilometre ring, then tracks south-west into the 400-kilometre zone on the 18th and 19th, eases away, and loops back into that closer range on the 21st before crossing Hainan and making landfall in Vietnam around the 22nd and 23rd.

The same model then sends a second, much larger cyclone past the Philippines into the South China Sea on the 22nd and 23rd, closing to within 400 kilometres of Hong Kong by the 24th. Together with other nearby systems, FengWu paints a rare "three cyclones dancing" scene.

FuXi is more direct. It brings a cyclone inside 800 kilometres on the 19th and 20th, into 400 kilometres on the 21st and 22nd, then ashore about 200 kilometres west of Hong Kong on the 22nd and 23rd. Pangu broadly agrees a storm is possible but differs on path and strength.

Locally, the Observatory expects White Dolphin to cross eastern China into the interior over the next day or two. Sinking air on its flanks should keep southern China extremely hot, with heat sparking thunderstorms. A south-westerly flow mid-week should keep Guangdong's coast baking with a few showers, before an upper disturbance late in the week adds rain and eases the extremes a little. The nine-day outlook still shows showers and thunderstorms on many days, temperatures from 27C to 36C, and winds no stronger than force 5 for now.
