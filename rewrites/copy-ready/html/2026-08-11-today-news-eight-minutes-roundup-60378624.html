11th August 2026 - (Hong Kong) A video news roundup pulled together several local incidents circulating online, from a football pitch brawl in Wong Tai Sin to a Pok Fu Lam knife attack, heat warnings under Super Typhoon White Dolphin and a village-house burglary crackdown.

Clips from Morse Park showed what looked like amateur sides turning a match into a fight after the referee showed a red card and a yellow card. In a second video players scuffled, one sat on the ground while another climbed on him, and punches and kicks followed amid shoving.

On the evening of 7th August at 10.12pm police received a report that a 50-year-old man's hand was trapped in a train door at Tiu Keng Leng station on the Tseung Kwan O Line. Rescuers freed him and took him to Tseung Kwan O Hospital. Police classified the case as accidental injury. It is understood his mobile phone fell onto the tracks and a hand reaching for it was caught; footage showed a bespectacled man in white kneeling with fingers jammed in a gap under the door, striking the glass and shouting for it to open while passengers calmed him and used the emergency intercom.

At Bel-Air in Pok Fu Lam a knife-wielding man injured a domestic helper and a male security guard. Reader video showed him approaching a woman on the road and stabbing her in the back. She ran, fell on the opposite carriageway, and as he moved in again an African man kicked him away while a security officer used a broom to keep him off, averting a worse outcome.

On the Island Line, Threads posts showed an elderly man shoving a girl from a priority seat. Her father filmed and challenged him, saying priority seats are for children too. The man raised his phone to film back, then left hastily after the father's pointed response; the girl smiled throughout.

Under White Dolphin's peripheral sinking air, extreme heat continued and the Labour Department kept a yellow working heat stress warning on 10th August. Site workers relied on fan shirts, cooling sleeves and litres of warm water or sports drinks. Some said a yellow alert near 40C felt too lenient; others said rest breaks hinged entirely on whether bosses allowed pauses.

Former Observatory director Lam Chiu-ying again called the guidance out of touch on a Commercial Radio show, saying outdoor sun exposure is not properly counted and that black-globe and wet-bulb temperatures should be used. He said the Observatory had prepared to release those readings but a department objected, fearing labour anxiety, leaving an index poorly suited to open-air work.

Separately, a Chinese national was jailed for forty-six years in Thailand on 4th August for illegal possession of heavy military weapons, though concurrent sentencing caps actual custody at twenty years. And on 8th August police arrested five people in a raid on a burglary ring active in Tuen Mun, Yuen Long and Tai Po, linking them to nineteen village-house break-ins from October last year to August with losses of about 2.1 million dollars, the largest single case about three hundred and fifty thousand. Investigators said the ringleader arranged two illegal mainland entrants — one allegedly a Wei Jia Gang member who arrived by sea — while two women handled stolen goods.
