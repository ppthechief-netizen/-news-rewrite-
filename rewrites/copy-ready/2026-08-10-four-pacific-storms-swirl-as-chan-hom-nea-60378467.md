10th August 2026 - (Hong Kong) Travellers bound for Japan should watch tropical storm Chan-hom, which is closing on the country while four systems dance across the western North Pacific.

China's Central Meteorological Observatory expects Chan-hom to hold intensity and make landfall in southern Ibaraki prefecture on Wednesday, 11th August, then cross northern Tokyo that evening. Japan's Meteorological Agency instead sees it strengthening to a severe tropical storm on Wednesday, coming ashore in northern Ibaraki, with possible effects on Nikko, Toyama and Kanazawa before it weakens to a tropical depression by Friday, 13th August, near south-eastern Korea. The Hong Kong Observatory's track probabilities likewise point toward the Kanto region.

White Dolphin made landfall in Zhejiang yesterday, 9th August, as a severe typhoon. In a noon weather update today, 10th August, the Observatory said it had weakened to a tropical storm, centred about 250 kilometres east of Nanchang at midday and moving north-west at about 26 kilometres an hour across inland eastern China while gradually losing strength.

Its morning track chart puts White Dolphin near eastern Wuhan by 8am tomorrow, 11th August, further weakened to a tropical depression with maximum sustained winds near 45 kilometres an hour, then to a low by Wednesday morning and expected to dissipate north-west of Wuhan.

People travelling north on the mainland should note the Central Meteorological Observatory's warning that White Dolphin, moving north-west at 15 to 20 kilometres an hour, will bring torrential rain to parts of eastern China, including north-eastern and north-western Zhejiang, north-western Jiangsu, central and northern Anhui, southern Henan and central and northern Hubei — with extreme torrential rain in northern Hubei.

On Chan-hom — a Lao name for a tree — the mainland centre said at 8am it lay about 950 kilometres east of Tokyo, moving north-west at 20 to 25 kilometres an hour with little change in strength toward Japan's eastern coast. Its chart keeps tropical-storm intensity until landfall in southern Ibaraki before 8pm on Wednesday, a westwards crossing of northern Tokyo, then Fukui by Thursday morning as it heads back to sea as a tropical depression.

Japan's agency charts a more northerly path: severe tropical storm status and northern Ibaraki landfall on Wednesday, then west through Nikko, Toyama and Kanazawa, easing to tropical storm strength on Thursday near Kanazawa in Ishikawa before returning to sea, and to a tropical depression near south-eastern Korea on Friday. The Observatory's probability cone is similar to Japan's and also favours Kanto.

Tropical storm Peilou — a Macao name for a common migratory bird — formed yesterday. The mainland centre said at 8am it was about 670 kilometres north-east of Saipan, racing north-east at about 35 kilometres an hour and likely to strengthen, with no expected impact on China; its chart has it weakening to a tropical depression tomorrow.

Farther east, another tropical depression has formed north-east of Peilou. If it reaches tropical-storm strength it will be named Nangka — Malaysia's jackfruit — but it remains far from Hong Kong and should not affect the city in the near term.
