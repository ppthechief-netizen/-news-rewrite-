11th August 2026 - (Hong Kong) Public transport plans for the soon-to-open new Huanggang Port will give inbound travellers three franchised bus options that can reach the Tai Lam Tunnel Bus-Bus Interchange in about 25 minutes, opening connections onto roughly 50 onward routes across the city.

The Transport and Logistics Bureau today set out the feeder network around the crossing: seven franchised bus routes, six green minibus routes, cross-boundary bus services and taxis. Officials said passengers entering through the new port can choose among three franchised buses for the short hop to Tai Lam, then continue towards west Kowloon, east Kowloon, Tsuen Wan, Tsing Yi and other districts on the dense web of connecting lines.

Tai Lam Tunnel Bus-Bus Interchange is cast as a pivotal hub for trips to and from the new Huanggang Port. The interchange already handles about 19,000 passenger trips a day on average. Routes 268X, 265B and 68X towards west Kowloon and Yau Tsim Mong are among the more popular choices, with peak-period frequencies of about every six to 12 minutes.

Secretary for Transport and Logistics Ms Mable Chan, JP, visited the interchange today to review preparations. She instructed the Transport Department and Kowloon Motor Bus to step up publicity and add clearer wayfinding, keep a close watch on ridership shifts across routes, and adjust frequencies when needed as the new port comes online.

The franchised bus package comprises seven routes, including one overnight service. Green minibuses will contribute six routes, three of them overnight. Cross-boundary coaches will include five short-haul services running around the clock. Taxi cover will span urban, New Territories and fleet taxis, giving travellers another way to leave the checkpoint without waiting for a scheduled departure.

For visitors unfamiliar with northwest New Territories transfers, the bureau's pitch is straightforward: clear the new Huanggang Port, board one of the three inbound franchised buses, and within about 25 minutes reach a tunnel interchange already woven into daily Kowloon and New Territories commuting. From there, the roughly 50 connecting routes are meant to disperse crowds towards employment centres, shopping districts and residential belts without forcing a single long trunk ride from the boundary.

Officials also signalled that monitoring will not stop at opening day. With Chan pressing operators to watch passenger flows and tweak timetables, the network is designed to flex if early demand concentrates on the west Kowloon corridors already favoured at Tai Lam, or if overnight and cross-boundary short-haul services need reinforcement once round-the-clock movements settle into a pattern.
