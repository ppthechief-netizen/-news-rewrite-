10th August 2026 - (Hong Kong) Eleven defendants from the 47-person Legislative Council primary case won certificates today allowing them to seek leave to appeal to the Court of Final Appeal after the Court of Appeal dismissed their conviction challenges earlier this year.

The wider prosecution charged forty-seven opposition figures over the 2020 unofficial primary with conspiracy to subvert state power. Thirty-one defendants, including Benny Tai Yiu-ting, who prosecutors cast as a principal architect, pleaded guilty. Fourteen others were convicted after trial. Among those convicted after contesting the case, Lam Cheuk-ting, Leung Kwok-hung and nine others appealed their convictions; Tiffany Yuen Ka-wai, who had pleaded guilty, appealed only her sentence; and the Department of Justice appealed the acquittal of Lawrence Lau Wai-chung. In February the Court of Appeal dismissed every side's applications.

The eleven refused conviction-appeal losers then asked for certificates to go upstairs. In a judgment handed down today the Court of Appeal granted certificates on five grounds so they may apply for leave from the top court. All eleven collected the judgment in person: Lam Cheuk-ting, Leung Kwok-hung, Helena Wong Pik-wan, Raymond Chan Chi-chuen, Owen Chow Ka-shing, Clarisse Yeung Suet-ying, Winnie Yu Huimin, Gwyneth Ho Kwai-lam, Ng Chi-hung, Cheng Tat-hung and Sam Cheung Ho-sum.

Their lawyers argue the case raises legal questions of great general or public importance that the Court of Final Appeal should settle. The five certified points ask how "other unlawful means" in Article 22 of the Hong Kong national security law should be read, including whether the ejusdem generis rule applies; whether the "scheme" defined by the Court of Appeal amounts to unlawful means under that article; whether the defined "course of conduct" is prohibited conduct under Article 22(3); whether the non-intervention principle applies on these facts; and whether a defendant who intends, for the purpose of that scheme and course of conduct, to trigger the mechanisms in Articles 50 to 52 of the Basic Law and their consequences harbours a specific subversive intent.

Forty-five defendants ultimately faced the conspiracy charge. The thirty-one who pleaded guilty drew prison terms from four years and two months to ten years, with Mr Tai receiving the heaviest sentence of ten years. Of sixteen who contested liability, Li Yu-hin and Mr Lau were acquitted; the rest were convicted and jailed for between six and a half years and seven years and nine months. Twelve people later appealed — ten on conviction and sentence, Ms Ho on conviction only, and Ms Yuen on sentence only. On 23rd February 2026 the Court of Appeal dismissed those twelve appeals and also dismissed the prosecution's challenge to Mr Lau's acquittal. Ms Yuen and the Department of Justice did not pursue further final appeals. The case reference is CACC 253/2024.
