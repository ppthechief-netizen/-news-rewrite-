10th August 2026 - (Hong Kong) A social-media post complaining that a steamed grouper and a roast duck landed on the same table at once has reopened a familiar Hong Kong argument: whether local restaurants still know how to pace a meal, or whether guests themselves fail to "call up" dishes.

The diner said service in the city lacks heart and claimed that at a familiar Chinese restaurant the two signature courses arrived together, forcing the awkward question of which to eat first. Five bottles of wine on the table were still unfinished when every other small dish also appeared at once, leaving the party unable to hold back the flood of plates.

Many readers sympathised. Serving duck and steamed fish together is a mistake, they wrote: cold duck skin softens and fat sets, while a cooled fish turns fishy and firm, so simultaneous arrival ruins both. Others swapped war stories of sugar water and fruit arriving before the fish, or yi mein noodles and fried rice opening the meal.

Some guessed restaurants once rushed courses to free tables when business was roaring, and now rush them to clock off when the dining room is quiet. A rival camp told the complainant to request "call-up" service — dishes held until the table signals — and mocked the idea that a walk-in guest deserves banquet-room pacing.

Whether the fault sits with slapdash kitchens or over-particular diners remains a matter of taste. What the thread does capture is a wider mood: that Hong Kong hospitality is fraying just as some diners look north for cheaper, keener service, and that course order — once a quiet craft of Cantonese banquets — has become another public grievance online.

For regulars and tourists alike, the exchange is a reminder that timing is part of the product: a crisp duck skin and a just-steamed fish each have a short window, and pouring everything at once wastes both the kitchen's work and the table's evening.
