10th August 2026 - (Hong Kong) A previously healthy 20-month-old boy is critically ill in the paediatric intensive care unit at Queen Elizabeth Hospital with influenza A complicated by septic shock, the Centre for Health Protection said today as it investigates the case.

Including this patient, Hong Kong has recorded seven severe paediatric influenza infections since the season began at the end of June, and one child has died. Officials expect flu activity to stay high for several more weeks and are urging households not to treat midsummer fever as a mild inconvenience.

The boy developed a fever last Friday, 7th August, and was taken to a private clinic the next day. He suffered a seizure at the clinic and was rushed to Queen Elizabeth Hospital's accident and emergency department, where low blood pressure led to admission to paediatric intensive care for urgent support.

Respiratory samples confirmed influenza A virus, with a clinical diagnosis of influenza A complicated by septic shock. He remains in hospital and is critical. Early inquiries show he did not receive last season's influenza vaccine and had no travel during the incubation period, so local community spread is the working assumption while contact tracing continues.

One household contact recently had upper-respiratory symptoms; a rapid antigen test detected influenza, but the illness was mild, needed no admission and has since resolved. The centre is continuing its investigation into how the virus moved through the household and whether other close contacts need monitoring.

Parents are again reminded that seasonal vaccination remains the best protection for young children before the virus peaks further, and that high fever, seizures or breathing trouble need prompt emergency care rather than watchful waiting at home. Clinics and hospitals are already seeing heavy paediatric demand as the summer wave holds across the city.
