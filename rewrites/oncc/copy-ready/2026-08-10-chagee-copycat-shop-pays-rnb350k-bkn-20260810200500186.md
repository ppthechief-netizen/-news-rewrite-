10th August 2026 - (Hong Kong) A Nanning intellectual property court has ordered a Guangxi catering firm to pay RMB350,000 (about HK$410,000) after finding trademark infringement and unfair competition against the registered Chagee tea brand, with the judgment already in force.

The plaintiff, a Beijing catering management company, holds registered marks covering Chagee, Cha Ji, Bo Ya Jue Xian and the distinctive female-head, letter and word combination associated with the chain.

From August last year the defendant opened a high-imitation outlet about twenty metres from a genuine Chagee shop in Laibin, copying the fascia, interior fit-out, staff uniforms and utensils while selling a drink that shared the star product name Bo Ya Jue Xian both in store and online.

Judges held that the defendant, without permission, took the distinctive "Cha Ji" element from the plaintiff's registered marks and used it as its own enterprise trade name — a classic "riding the brand" move likely to confuse traders and consumers alike across the street.

Weighing fault and profit, the court ordered compensation for economic loss plus reasonable enforcement costs and told the defendant to publish a clarifying statement for no fewer than seven days to erase the lingering effect of the deception on the local market.

The ruling shows how sharply mainland courts now police lookalike tea outlets that sit next door to national brands and recycle signature drink titles. Chagee, known in Chinese as Bawang Chaji, has expanded rapidly across the region, and shops that borrow logos, uniforms and product names now face six-figure yuan awards once rights holders sue and prove consumer confusion in nearby locations. Industry watchers say proximity matters: opening a near-identical shop within twenty metres of a flagship, then selling a drink under the same star name, made the confusion case unusually clear for the Nanning bench.
