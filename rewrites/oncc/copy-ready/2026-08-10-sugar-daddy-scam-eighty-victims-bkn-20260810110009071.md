10th August 2026 - (Hong Kong) Officers smashed what they describe as an escort-style sugar-daddy fraud ring after deep inquiries, launching Operation Strict Net last Tuesday, 4th August, and arresting four men and four women aged twenty-three to fifty-seven.

Charges under consideration include conspiracy to defraud, money laundering and possession of dangerous drugs. Police link the group to eighty cases involving more than HK$6.2 million, with the largest single loss put at HK$480,000. Phones and SIM cards were seized during the raids. The alleged ringleader and a core member have been provisionally charged with conspiracy to defraud and money laundering, appeared in court on 5th August, and were remanded pending a further hearing on 28th October; the other detainees were released on bail pending further inquiries.

Investigators say the ring has been active since February 2024, dangling monthly "kept" payments of HK$50,000 to HK$70,000 on dating platforms to lure targets. Once victims joined messaging groups, members allegedly posed as a wealthy patron and a lawyer, flashing forged lawyer cards to look professional and trustworthy. Targets were then told a confidential kept-woman agreement required deposits of legal fees and penalty sums into nominated accounts, with a promise the money would be returned in full afterwards. After the transfers cleared, contact vanished without the promised repayments.

Some victims faced a second sting in which fraudsters claimed they could help recover the earlier loss — for another handling fee — deepening the financial damage. Of the eighty victims, about 65 per cent were women. Ages ranged from nineteen to forty-one across many trades: more than 52 per cent worked in services, retail or clerical support, 20 per cent were students, and about 15 per cent were professionals such as teachers and medical staff.

Police urge the public to treat high-pay dating offers and requests for upfront legal deposits with extreme caution and to report similar approaches promptly, noting that the operation remains under follow-up inquiry for the bailed suspects.
