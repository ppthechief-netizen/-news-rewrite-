10th August 2026 - (Hong Kong) A thirty-year-old man who pleaded guilty to inciting others to disclose personal data without consent has won a sentencing appeal, walking away with the same two-month term suspended for two years instead of serving it immediately.

Deputy High Court Judge Cheung Kit-yee handed down the ruling today after an earlier hearing. The appellant, Chan Yik-lam, thirty, who works at the A.S. Watson Group on a monthly salary of HK$35,000, had admitted one count of inciting disclosure of personal data without the data subject's consent and was jailed for two months. He argued the sentence was too heavy and sought a softer disposal.

Police found online discussion in May 2024 about a concluded indecent-assault case in which a male defendant had been acquitted. Part of a post urged readers to publish personal details of a woman involved in the matter. After further inquiries officers arrested Mr Chan last March, saying he had replied in May 2024 that he hoped someone might accidentally reveal the woman's particulars. The comment drew six likes and no replies, the court heard.

On appeal he argued the magistrate wrongly refused to consider suspension or a community service order, wrongly rejected a claim of a momentary slip, that the term was excessive, and that two co-defendants who also pleaded guilty received community service — a stark disparity. Judge Cheung held the post reflected deliberate thinking, not an accidental blurt, and agreed with the magistrate that cases interfering with justice, public interest, the rule of law and confidence in the courts normally demand deterrent immediate custody.

But she weighed his guilty plea, clean record and good background, plus the six likes and lack of any reply showing further harm flowing from his words, and found suspension appropriate while still marking the seriousness of doxxing-style incitement. The case number is HCMA 315/2025.
