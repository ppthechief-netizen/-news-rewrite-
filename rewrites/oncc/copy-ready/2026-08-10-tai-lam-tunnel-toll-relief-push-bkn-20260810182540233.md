10th August 2026 - (Hong Kong) Drivers and a New Territories North West lawmaker are pressing for deeper Tai Lam Tunnel cuts after the government, having taken the tunnel back and introduced time-band tolls, completed a review that leaves private-car charges unchanged at HK$18 off-peak to HK$45 at the rush — down from the old flat HK$58 but still too steep for many.

Miss Ho, who drives a light goods vehicle, said she now prefers Tuen Mun Road despite longer journeys simply to save money. Mr Yip vowed never to use the tunnel outside the HK$18 window, content to sit in traffic with music playing rather than pay peak rates. Mr Ngan argued that if Tai Lam costs more than other tunnels whose upkeep should be similar, the gap is unjustified.

Legislator Mark Chong Ho-fung noted Transport Department figures showing daily throughput rising from about 46,000 vehicles before the new tolls to roughly 60,000 afterwards, much of it shifted from Tuen Mun Road. He supports peak pricing to push some trips onto public transport and ease twin jams, but says diversion can go further.

Citing LegCo papers, he said annual revenue after the takeover is about HK$600 million against operating costs near HK$390 million, including HK$70 million of depreciation on a tunnel the government did not build. Strip that depreciation and the surplus approaches HK$280 million — room, he argues, for free night off-peak travel and a cut in ordinary periods from HK$30 to HK$15, because off-peak hours no longer need a diversion policy.

He linked high tolls to worse Tuen Mun town-centre congestion as Shenzhen Bay and Liantang cross-boundary lorries hauling high-value airport cargo avoid Tai Lam and clog local roads. Using HKeToll, he suggested rebating half or all of a lorry's Tai Lam charge if the same plate reaches the airport via Tsing Ma within about thirty minutes, creating a clear incentive for airport-bound trucks. Toll reviews, he added, still lack a fixed timetable and usually wait for fierce district or LegCo pressure — as shown by the still-unadjusted harbour-crossing diversion charges.
