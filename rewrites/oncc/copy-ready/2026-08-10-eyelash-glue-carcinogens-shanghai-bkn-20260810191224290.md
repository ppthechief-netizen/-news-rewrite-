10th August 2026 - (Hong Kong) Beauty seekers who stick or graft false lashes often complain of itchy, burning or watering eyes — and Shanghai's consumer council now says the glue itself may be the culprit after sending 28 hot-selling adhesives for lab tests.

More than 80 per cent of samples contained formaldehyde, hydroquinone or other carcinogens banned from cosmetics, and some formulas closely matched industrial "502" cyanoacrylate glue, mainland state media reported, raising alarms for salons and home users alike.

Reporters walking into a lash salon in Jing'an were hit by a sharp chemical smell. Staff shrugged that most glues reek and even treated a stronger odour as proof the bond is firm. Asked for brand, origin and a traceable filing number, answers stayed vague; if a client reacts badly, the shop simply tells them to go to hospital themselves rather than documenting the product.

Follow-up traced some problem products to Pingdu in Qingdao, Shandong — a hub for false lashes and adhesives long said to operate with "no standard and nobody watching". Several makers admitted lash glue sits outside ordinary cosmetics rules and lacks dedicated controls, that formaldehyde is an industry default, and that allergy claims usually end in token payouts.

One firm even said a full quality-test pack and execution standard could be bought for about RMB600 (roughly HK$700). Regulators and shoppers now face a market where beauty-counter convenience outruns safety paperwork, and where "strong hold" may simply mean industrial chemistry on the eyelid.

Shoppers are advised to demand clear ingredient lists and filing numbers before any lash appointment, and to walk away from salons that treat a piercing chemical smell as a badge of strength rather than a warning about banned substances near the eye. Mainland media said the probe is still expanding today.
