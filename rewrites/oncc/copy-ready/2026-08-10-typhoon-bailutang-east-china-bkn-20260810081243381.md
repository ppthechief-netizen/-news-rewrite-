10th August 2026 - (Hong Kong) Tropical storm Bailutang — known in Chinese reports as White Dolphin — made two landfalls on Sunday evening in Taizhou and Wenzhou in Zhejiang while still packing typhoon strength, with maximum winds near the centre rated at force 14.

Mainland agencies issued cascading alerts for mountain torrents, rainstorms, severe convection, waterlogging, floods and geological hazards. China's National Meteorological Centre said landfall winds briefly reached about 151 kilometres an hour, roughly a Category 1 hurricane on the US Saffir-Simpson scale. Reports put East China rail and air cancellations at a large scale and said more than one million people were evacuated, though those figures diverge from some official tallies.

In Yueqing, Wenzhou, rescuers used electric saws to free vehicles trapped by fallen trees. A telecom engineering truck stalled in floodwater under a bridge nearly half a person deep; crew escaped through the rear before a recovery vehicle hauled the van clear. Zhejiang's water resources authorities said the Yongjiang basin around Ningbo logged 158 millimetres of rain and the eastern Hangjiahu plain 104.8 millimetres, with both the Yongjiang and canal systems recording their first floods of the year that night. One Yuhuan resident filmed a live small octopus stuck to a fourth-floor window about a kilometre inland; a Shanghai netizen photographed what looked like thick salmon sashimi plastered to home glass — vivid signs of how far the gale flung coastal debris.

By 6.00pm Sunday, flight-tracking platform VariFlight showed more than 2,200 cancellations at Shanghai Hongqiao and Pudong, with over 80 per cent of movements halted and nearly 60 per cent of Sunday schedules already cut. Sichuan and Chongqing airports cancelled more than 600 flights between them. Shanghai expected roughly another 40 per cent of Monday arrivals and departures to be trimmed, with 943 planned cancellations. An Air China Chengdu-to-Hongqiao service diverted to Beijing Capital after circling Shanghai, returned to Chengdu that night, and some passengers gave up and reached the city by high-speed rail only after 10.00pm.

On Monday Shanghai suspended Metro lines 3, 5, 16 and the Pujiang line entirely, shortened other routes, halted waterborne passenger services, and prepared to close the Yangtze and Donghai bridges if winds topped force 9. In Taiwan, the disaster response centre counted 632 general incidents by 2.00pm Sunday, mostly in the north — Taipei 277, New Taipei 177, Keelung 84 and Taoyuan 59 — with six people injured island-wide by 8.00am as the same weather system brushed the region.
