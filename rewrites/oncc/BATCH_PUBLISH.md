10th August 2026 - (Hong Kong) The Home Affairs Department said on social media that the government began an upgrade of Wo Tsai Shan — better known as Bishop Hill in Sham Shui Po — today, promising a twin lift in facilities and safety so local residents get better recreation and open space.

Illegal occupation of government land on the hill has been a stubborn district headache for more than a decade, the department said. Officials framed the response as firm legality tempered with community need: clear unsafe and unlawful structures while answering demand for sport and rest space, ending with safer, better amenities for people who use the slopes.

Clearance will run in two phases. The first covers the hillside resting area along Tong Yam Street from the foot of the hill to the summit; the second will seal the stretch from the Barnet Street side of the slope up to the peak. The Lands Department has already posted statutory notices for first-phase facilities and structures, telling owners to stop occupying the land by 12th August and urging them to dismantle or remove their own property quickly.

Each phase will fence off roughly one side of Wo Tsai Shan for about three days so crews can work safely. During those windows the other entrance will stay open for walks and for access to the former Sham Shui Po Service Reservoir at the top. Once a stage is finished, the site will reopen and the government will move straight into seven upgrade packages on safe, suitable spots: repairing directional signs; placing comfortable seating in safe positions instead of rough tile-built benches, some of them perched near slope edges; improving shade shelters with seats, roof and foundation repairs; mending cut railings on paths; replacing ageing, uneven fitness gear with new equipment and soft safety mats; carrying out slope repairs, protection and ground levelling; and adding table-tennis tables both on safe hill sites and at Tai Hang Tung Playground.

Depending on each project's pace, officials hope the upgrades will land between late August and sometime in September. Religious objects already on the hill will be treated with particular respect: the government has invited religious leaders to help handle them properly, including dignified rites and relocation to suitable places, rather than treating faith items as ordinary debris.

10th August 2026 - (Hong Kong) A thirty-year-old man who pleaded guilty to inciting others to disclose personal data without consent has won a sentencing appeal, walking away with the same two-month term suspended for two years instead of serving it immediately.

Deputy High Court Judge Cheung Kit-yee handed down the ruling today after an earlier hearing. The appellant, Chan Yik-lam, thirty, who works at the A.S. Watson Group on a monthly salary of HK$35,000, had admitted one count of inciting disclosure of personal data without the data subject's consent and was jailed for two months. He argued the sentence was too heavy and sought a softer disposal.

Police found online discussion in May 2024 about a concluded indecent-assault case in which a male defendant had been acquitted. Part of a post urged readers to publish personal details of a woman involved in the matter. After further inquiries officers arrested Mr Chan last March, saying he had replied in May 2024 that he hoped someone might accidentally reveal the woman's particulars. The comment drew six likes and no replies, the court heard.

On appeal he argued the magistrate wrongly refused to consider suspension or a community service order, wrongly rejected a claim of a momentary slip, that the term was excessive, and that two co-defendants who also pleaded guilty received community service — a stark disparity. Judge Cheung held the post reflected deliberate thinking, not an accidental blurt, and agreed with the magistrate that cases interfering with justice, public interest, the rule of law and confidence in the courts normally demand deterrent immediate custody.

But she weighed his guilty plea, clean record and good background, plus the six likes and lack of any reply showing further harm flowing from his words, and found suspension appropriate while still marking the seriousness of doxxing-style incitement. The case number is HCMA 315/2025.
