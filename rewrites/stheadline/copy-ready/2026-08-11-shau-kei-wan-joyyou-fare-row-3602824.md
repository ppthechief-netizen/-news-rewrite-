11th August 2026 - (Hong Kong) An elderly woman in Shau Kei Wan posted in a local Facebook group after her JoyYou Card was charged $2.20 for what she said was only a one- or two-stop bus trip towards Chai Wan, asking neighbours whether the debit was a mistake.

Writing on the Shau Kei Wan and Sai Wan Ho Concern Group page, she said she boarded at Nam On Lane because she was carrying too many bags for the short walk. She expected sympathy; instead the thread turned into a public lesson on how the government's revised elderly concession works.

Readers quickly acted as human calculators. Several stressed that the charge depends on the route's full adult fare, not how many stops a passenger rides. Under the "two dollars or two-fold" scheme, trips whose adult fare tops $10 are billed at 20 per cent — so $2.20 implies an adult fare of about $11. She first insisted the ordinary fare should be only around $5.

One commenter spelled out that cross-harbour or other long-route services keep a higher base price even after island sectional displays show smaller figures. Boarding at Nam On Lane does not reset that base: if the adult fare already exceeds $10, a single stop can still cost $2.20. A sharper reply guessed she took route 14 towards Stanley, where boarding at Nam On Lane normally costs $12.1 — two-fold equals $2.20, with the government topping up about $9.9.

Once that maths landed, criticism hardened into accusations of "long bus, short ride" — boarding an expensive long route for a tiny hop and still claiming victimhood. Posts accused her of taking a subsidy then alleging she was cheated, while one sarcastically suggested she walk next time rather than fuss over twenty cents.

She pushed back that she had paid taxes for decades and was entitled to use the policy, saying she only wanted clarity on non-cross-harbour pricing. On the walking jab she replied that climbing from Nam On Lane up to Chai Wan Road with heavy bags is hard for older legs. When someone urged her to call police or a TV consumer show over twenty cents, she answered earnestly that officers would not open a case for that sum — a straight reply that only fuelled the comedy.

Amid the row, another user pasted a mock ghost-bus yarn — long-haired silent passengers, a motionless driver — to spook her after she could not recall the route number. She played along: she would rather meet a ghost than a thief.

She later cooled the thread, thanked measured advice, admitted she had wrongly thought the two-dollar two-fold rule applied only after reaching Kowloon, and said she now understood some Hong Kong Island long fares need separate tap-on and tap-off for the cheapest elderly rate. She promised to check the displayed charge before boarding next time.

The exchange has become another viral primer on JoyYou maths: section length does not decide the concession — the adult fare of the bus you choose does.
