10th August 2026 - (Shenzhen) Shenzhen marked the 100-day countdown to the 33rd Asia-Pacific Economic Cooperation Economic Leaders' Meeting on Monday, putting the host city's green planning choices back under regional scrutiny.

Decades ago the southern megacity made an unusual planning decision in Futian District, shifting a planned coastal road 260 metres north to spare the original mangrove forest. Bao Daming, secretary-general of the International Mangrove Center secretariat based in Shenzhen, said the move was intended to protect that woodland and still stands as a symbol of how the city tries to balance growth with conservation.

That approach was on show at the 2026 APEC Ministerial Meeting on Forestry held in Shenzhen recently. Representatives from 21 economies discussed sustainable forest management, mangrove and blue carbon conservation, urban biodiversity, smart ecological governance and legal trade in forest products, and they signed a joint statement setting directions for deeper regional cooperation.

As the countdown boards went up — including a display at Shenzhen Bay Sports Center — the city opened sites involving mangrove protection, river restoration and ecological corridors to visiting forestry officials and technical teams from APEC economies. Through the International Mangrove Center it is also pushing joint research on cross-border mangrove restoration and blue carbon standards.

Shenzhen has built a citywide ecological network linking mountain forests, rivers and coastal wetlands. More than 20 ecological corridors, including the Kunpeng Trail, have helped reconnect fragmented urban habitats where leopard cats and Eurasian otters have returned. More than 6,870 species have been recorded across the city, including 208 species of nationally protected plants and animals.

In the central urban area, the Futian mangrove nature reserve is a key stopover for migratory birds on the East Asian-Australasian Flyway, and an artificial-intelligence smart protection system helps safeguard tens of thousands of birds. Eduardo Pedrosa, executive director of the APEC Secretariat, said Shenzhen's mix of towers and greenery, including the protected mangrove, could serve as a reference for cities across the Asia-Pacific. "It's interesting to see how Shenzhen has really developed," he said.

Camila Isabel Zepeda Lizama, who heads international affairs at Mexico's environment ministry, said she was keen on China's use of information technology, robotics and artificial intelligence to pick restoration sites. "We're very interested in cooperating with China," she said, pointing to mangrove restoration, forest monitoring and blue carbon sequestration.

Beyond wetlands, Shenzhen is pursuing green industrial change, digital-energy industries and citywide parks. More than 90 per cent of the 310 rivers under monitoring have good or excellent water quality. A bilingual white paper released on 3rd August, titled "A City of Symbiosis: The Shenzhen Practice of Harmonious Coexistence between Humanity and Nature," outlines four pathways for urban ecological governance and presents 19 representative cases. Bao said the center plans to share Shenzhen's experience with members worldwide as APEC season intensifies.
