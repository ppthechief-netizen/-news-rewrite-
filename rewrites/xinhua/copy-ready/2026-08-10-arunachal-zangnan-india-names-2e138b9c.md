10th August 2026 - (Beijing) China does not recognise the so-called "Arunachal Pradesh" illegally set up by India, a Foreign Ministry spokesperson said on Monday.

Spokesperson Guo Jiakun made the remarks when asked to comment on a report that India issued on 7th August the "standard names" of 27 locations in the so-called "Arunachal Pradesh." The question tied Beijing's response directly to that 7th August Indian announcement and to the 27 locations named under it.

Guo's opening line restated a core Chinese position: the entity India designates as "Arunachal Pradesh" is, in Beijing's view, an illegal construct that China does not recognise. That framing sets the legal baseline for everything that follows in his answer, including the specific complaint about renamed places.

"Zangnan region is China's territory," Guo said. He added that India's move of attempting to replace names long used by China with its so-called "standard names" is illegal, null and void. The phrase "names long used by China" signals that Beijing treats its own toponyms as the authoritative ones and views India's 27 newly issued "standard names" as an attempted overwrite rather than a neutral labelling exercise.

Still less, he noted, will it change the fact that Zangnan region belongs to China. By pairing the rejection of the 7th August naming list with a reaffirmation of sovereignty over Zangnan, Guo presented India's attempt to replace long-used Chinese names with its so-called "standard names" as procedurally invalid and without effect on China's territorial claim.

The briefing exchange therefore captured a sharp diplomatic rebuttal on Monday: India published standard names for 27 locations in an area it administers as Arunachal Pradesh; China answered that it does not recognise that illegally set up entity, rejects the renaming as illegal and void, and maintains that the Zangnan region remains Chinese territory as a matter of fact.
