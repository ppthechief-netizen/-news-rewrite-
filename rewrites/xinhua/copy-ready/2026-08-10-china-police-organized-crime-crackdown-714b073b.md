10th August 2026 - (Beijing) Chinese police have dismantled more than 1,000 criminal groups and arrested over 8,200 suspects in a nationwide operation against organised crime, especially gangs using increasingly covert and evolving tactics, the Ministry of Public Security said on Monday.

The operation also uncovered more than 5,400 criminal cases across the country, the ministry said, framing the haul as evidence that specialised campaigns can still disrupt networks even as offenders shift methods.

In recent years police have kept a hard line against organised crime, arguing that sustained pressure has helped curb such offences and support a broader improvement in public security. Yet the ministry warned that some groups have moved onto online platforms and used non-violent forms of coercion to commit crimes and expand quickly, making traditional street-level disruption alone insufficient.

New forms of organised crime highlighted by officials include online predatory lending schemes, coercive consumption and cyberbullying — patterns that can trap victims through debt, forced spending or digital harassment rather than classic street violence. Those case types have become increasingly prominent in recent years, according to the ministry's account of the threat picture.

The ministry pledged to keep taking the initiative, swiftly cracking down on criminal groups as they appear and organising regular nationwide operations against such offences. That language signals a standing campaign posture rather than a one-off sweep timed to a single holiday or congress cycle.

China has recorded a decline in crime in recent years while people's sense of safety has continued to rise, officials say. National data show criminal cases fell 12.8 per cent year on year in 2025 to the lowest level since 2000, while public security cases declined 3.5 per cent — figures the ministry places alongside Monday's organised-crime results as part of the same enforcement story.

By pairing mass group dismantlements with a focus on online lending, coercive consumption and cyberbullying, police are trying to close the gap between falling traditional crime counts and the newer, quieter ways gangs recruit, intimidate and extract money. Monday's totals — more than 1,000 groups broken, over 8,200 people arrested and more than 5,400 cases filed — set a high bar for how that dual track will be judged in the months ahead.
