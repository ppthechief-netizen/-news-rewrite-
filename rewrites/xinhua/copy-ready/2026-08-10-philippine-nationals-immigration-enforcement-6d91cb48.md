10th August 2026 - (Beijing) Lawful action has been taken against more than 100 Philippine nationals for illegally working or living in China, a foreign ministry spokesperson said on Monday.

Spokesperson Guo Jiakun made the remarks when answering a related media question. His account pointed to a cluster of cases involving Philippine passport holders who were found to be working or residing in the country without lawful status, triggering enforcement steps taken in accordance with the law rather than outside it.

Guo said China's national immigration authorities have made consistent efforts to strengthen supervision of foreigners illegally entering, living or working in China. That supervision, he indicated, is not limited to a single inspection drive but forms part of ongoing administrative work aimed at identifying people who lack proper entry, residence or employment authorisation across the country.

He added that the authorities have carried out special operations in accordance with the law as part of that broader effort. The reference to special operations indicates targeted campaigns alongside routine checks, with enforcement directed at people who enter, live or work in China without lawful permission.

While carrying out the special operations, China's immigration staff acted in a standard and civil manner, Guo said. He stressed that officials ensured the protection of the rights and interests of the foreign nationals concerned even as enforcement proceeded, framing the process as lawful, procedurally controlled and carried out by national immigration authorities rather than unconstrained.

Taken together, Guo's remarks linked the more than 100 Philippine nationals to a wider immigration enforcement agenda that covers illegal entry, illegal residence and illegal employment. By highlighting both strengthened supervision and the conduct standards applied during those operations, the ministry sought to show that enforcement remains active while rights safeguards stay part of the process for the foreign nationals concerned.
