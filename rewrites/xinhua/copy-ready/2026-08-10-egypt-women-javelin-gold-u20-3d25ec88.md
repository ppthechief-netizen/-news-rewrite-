10th August 2026 - (Cairo) Egyptian athlete Asil Osama has won the women's javelin throw gold medal at the World Athletics U20 Championships in Oregon, the US, claiming her country's first ever medal in a women's event at the tournament.

Osama secured the title with a throw of 59.82 metres, according to the Egyptian Ministry of Youth and Sports on Monday. The distance gave her the top step on the podium at the under-20 global meeting held on American soil, where Egypt had never before reached the podium in a women's discipline at this championship level.

The ministry's announcement framed the victory as a landmark for Egyptian athletics rather than a routine international result. Until Osama's throw, no Egyptian woman had stood on the medal rostrum at the World Athletics U20 Championships, making the javelin gold a first for the programme across the full span of women's events at the tournament.

Egyptian Minister of Youth and Sports Jawhar Nabil congratulated Osama on the achievement, extending praise to the Egyptian Athletics Federation and to the team's technical, medical and administrative staff. The minister's message underscored that the medal was treated as a collective success for the federation and the support structure travelling with the squad in Oregon.

The win arrives with Egypt's senior and age-group programmes seeking broader recognition on the world stage, and the ministry's public congratulations signal official pride in a result produced outside Cairo. Osama's 59.82-metre winning effort stands as the measurable mark of the breakthrough, while the broader significance rests on the historical first in a women's event at the U20 world championships.

For Egyptian sport, Monday's bulletin from the youth and sports ministry fixed both the performance and its context: a gold medal in women's javelin at the World Athletics U20 Championships, a winning throw of 59.82 metres, and a first women's medal for the country at the tournament. Nabil's congratulations to Osama, the federation and the backroom staff closed the official account of a result that rewrites Egypt's record book at the under-20 worlds.
