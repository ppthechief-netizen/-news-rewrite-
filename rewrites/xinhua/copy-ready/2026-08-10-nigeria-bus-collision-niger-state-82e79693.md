10th August 2026 - (Abuja) A total of 15 people were killed and 25 others injured after two commercial buses collided in Nigeria's central state of Niger over the weekend, the traffic police said on Monday.

Aishatu Sa'adu, sector commander of the Federal Road Safety Corps in Niger, said by telephone that the two buses caught fire following the collision along the Enagi-Mokwa road late on Saturday. The fire after impact compounded the crash itself, turning a high-speed collision into a blaze that left 15 dead and dozens hurt before help could fully contain the scene.

Citing a preliminary investigation, Sa'adu attributed the accident to "wrongful overtaking at a bend," saying the two buses were travelling at high speed. The combination of an illegal overtaking manoeuvre on a curve and excessive speed forms the core of the early official explanation, rather than a finding of mechanical failure or weather alone.

The collision on the Enagi-Mokwa corridor therefore joins a familiar pattern of commercial passenger-vehicle disasters on Nigerian intercity routes, where bends and competitive driving can leave little margin for error. With both vehicles described as commercial buses, the passenger load would have raised the potential casualty count once the wrecks ignited.

Fatal road accidents are common in Nigeria and are often attributed to speeding, overloading, poor road conditions and reckless driving. Sa'adu's preliminary account of wrongful overtaking at a bend and high speed fits squarely within that broader risk profile that safety agencies repeatedly cite when explaining weekend and intercity tolls.

Monday's briefing fixed the human cost at 15 killed and 25 injured, located the crash on the Enagi-Mokwa road in Niger state late on Saturday, and named wrongful overtaking at a bend under high speed as the working cause. Until a fuller inquiry revises those findings, that remains the Federal Road Safety Corps' account of how two commercial buses collided, caught fire and produced one of the state's latest mass-casualty road disasters.
