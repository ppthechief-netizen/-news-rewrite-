10th August 2026 - (Ouagadougou) The price of diesel in Burkina Faso will rise from 675 to 750 CFA francs, about 1.32 US dollars per litre, starting Monday, Abdou-Salam Gampene, secretary-general of the Burkinabe prime minister's office and chairman of the interministerial committee for determining hydrocarbon prices, has announced.

The increase follows nearly four years during which diesel stayed at 675 CFA francs per litre, with the last adjustment made in August 2022, Gampene told national broadcaster RTB on Sunday. Monday's move therefore ends a long stretch of frozen pump prices even as global energy and currency conditions shifted around the fixed domestic tariff.

He said the decision was driven mainly by a surge in international oil prices, rising land and maritime transportation costs, and the appreciation of the US dollar against the CFA franc. Those pressures raised the underlying cost of supplying diesel while the retail price remained unchanged until this week.

According to Gampene, the actual cost of diesel once reached about 1,050 CFA francs per litre, while government subsidies for diesel amounted to about 60 billion CFA francs during the first six months of 2026. Without corrective measures, subsidies could exceed 135 billion CFA francs by the end of the year, he added.

Gampene also noted that diesel remained cheaper in Burkina Faso than in most neighbouring countries, fuelling higher consumption, particularly from an influx of foreign transporters refuelling in the country. Diesel consumption exceeded 365 million litres by the end of June and could reach 730 million litres by the end of the year, compared with an estimated national average consumption of about 500 million litres, he said.

The difference, estimated at more than 230 million litres, would represent a burden of about 70 billion CFA francs for the state, the official said, warning that the subsidy system could thus indirectly benefit consumers and transporters from neighbouring countries. That pressure, he added, is straining the financial sustainability of the subsidy system and could jeopardise the continuity of Burkina Faso's hydrocarbon supplies.
