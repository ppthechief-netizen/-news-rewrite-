10th August 2026 - (Damascus) An Israeli military patrol advanced into the Abu Qbeis Hill area in the countryside of Syria's southwestern Quneitra province on Monday, Syrian state media reported.

The patrol consisted of two tanks and several military vehicles carrying soldiers, state-run Al-Ikhbariya TV reported. The account identified the Abu Qbeis Hill area in Quneitra's countryside as the focus of Monday's Israeli movement.

The latest incursion came a day after an Israeli force entered the village of Eastern Samadaniyah in Quneitra with two tanks and several military vehicles carrying soldiers, according to the report. Both the Sunday and Monday operations were described as involving the same mix of armour and troop-carrying vehicles.

Following Sunday's incursion, Israeli forces conducted sweeping operations in the village's agricultural areas, during which gunfire was heard and searches were carried out, the report said. Those operations in Eastern Samadaniyah's farmland followed the initial entry into the village documented by Al-Ikhbariya a day before the Abu Qbeis Hill advance.

Syria has repeatedly accused Israel of violating the 1974 Disengagement Agreement through incursions into southern Syria, raids, detentions, land-clearing operations and shelling. The state media report on Monday's patrol and Sunday's activity in Eastern Samadaniyah was presented within that broader catalogue of alleged violations along the disengagement line.

Al-Ikhbariya tied the back-to-back movements in Quneitra province to the pattern of Israeli military presence that Syrian officials say breaches the 1974 pact. Damascus has long framed such incursions, alongside raids, detentions, land-clearing work and shelling in southern Syria, as contravening the disengagement arrangements reached in 1974.

The Monday advance into Abu Qbeis Hill and the prior sweep through Eastern Samadaniyah's agricultural areas, with gunfire heard during searches, marked the latest entries reported by Syrian state television in the southwestern province. State-run Al-Ikhbariya TV carried both accounts as part of its coverage of Israeli military movements in Quneitra.
