10th August 2026 - (Jakarta) More than 107,000 hectares of land and forest across Indonesia had been affected by fires as of early August, the government said on Monday, as drought conditions linked to a strong El Nino pattern continue to fan the blazes.

Coordinating Minister for Political and Security Affairs Djamari Chaniago told a press conference that authorities had deployed both air and ground operations to fight the fires. The air component currently involves about 43 helicopters and 10 to 15 fixed-wing aircraft, with the exact mix adjusted according to operational need.

That scale of aerial response underlines how widely the hotspot map has spread. Helicopters are typically used for water bombing, crew insertion and rapid reconnaissance over inaccessible peat and forest tracts, while fixed-wing aircraft can cover longer distances between provinces when multiple fronts ignite at once.

Djamari said the fires had been exacerbated by a prolonged drought driven by a strong El Nino weather pattern. El Nino years often bring below-normal rainfall across large parts of the archipelago, drying fuels on the forest floor and in drained peatlands that can smoulder for days once ignited.

Early August tallies already above 107,000 hectares signal a serious start to the dry-season fire year rather than a handful of isolated incidents. Land and forest loss on that scale threatens livelihoods, air quality in nearby towns and carbon-rich ecosystems, especially where peat soils burn beneath the surface.

Officials did not publish a province-by-province breakdown in the Monday briefing, nor a full count of ground crews, but the dual air-and-ground posture and the El Nino drought warning make clear that the campaign is being run as a national security and environmental priority. Further updates are expected as satellites and field teams refine the burned-area estimate through the rest of August.

Communities in fire-prone districts will continue to face haze, disrupted farming and the risk of new spot fires while dry weather holds. For now, the confirmed picture is stark: more than 107,000 hectares already affected, scores of aircraft in the air, and a government linking the crisis directly to a powerful El Nino drought.
