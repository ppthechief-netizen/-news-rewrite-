10th August 2026 - (Bangkok) Trade between Thailand and its free trade agreement partners rose 21.8 per cent year on year to 243.67 billion US dollars in the first half of 2026, accounting for 57.3 per cent of the country's total trade, official data showed on Monday.

According to the Ministry of Commerce, exports to the 18 FTA partners gained 12.6 per cent to 105.49 billion dollars during the January-June period, while imports climbed 30 per cent to 138.18 billion dollars. The import surge outpaced export growth by a wide margin, leaving the FTA basket tilted toward inbound shipments even as overall two-way trade expanded strongly.

China remained Thailand's largest FTA trading partner, followed by Japan, Malaysia, Vietnam, Singapore and India, the ministry said in a statement. That ranking places East and Southeast Asian economies at the centre of Thailand's preferential trade map, with India also among the leading partners by volume in the first half.

Chotima Iemsawasdikul, director general of the ministry's Department of Trade Negotiations, said that despite geopolitical tensions, protectionist measures and supply-chain uncertainty, the agreements had remained a key mechanism for opening markets, linking production chains and securing industrial raw materials. Her assessment framed the FTA network as a buffer for Thai industry when global trade conditions turn more fragmented.

Chotima said the faster import growth reflected demand for production inputs, as more than 75 per cent consisted of raw materials, intermediate goods and capital goods used to raise productivity and add value to Thai output. In that reading, the 30 per cent jump in FTA imports is less a pure consumption story and more a signal that factories are drawing in materials and equipment under preferential terms.

Taken together, the first-half figures show FTA partners absorbing more than half of Thailand's total trade — 57.3 per cent — while China leads the partner list and imports of industrial inputs dominate the inbound side. With exports to the 18 partners at 105.49 billion dollars and imports at 138.18 billion dollars, the ministry's data underscore both the scale of preferential commerce and the role of free-trade pacts in feeding Thai production chains amid unsettled geopolitics and protectionist pressures.
