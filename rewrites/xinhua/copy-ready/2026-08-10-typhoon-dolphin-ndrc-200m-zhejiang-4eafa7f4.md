10th August 2026 - (Beijing) China has urgently allocated fresh central funding for eastern regions hit by torrential rain and severe flooding from Typhoon Dolphin, the 13th typhoon of the year, as forecasts warn remnant rains will push further north.

The National Development and Reform Commission said on Monday it had allocated 200 million yuan (about 29.46 million US dollars) from the central budget to support disaster recovery in Zhejiang Province. The money is earmarked mainly for emergency restoration of damaged infrastructure, including roads and water conservancy works, and for public service facilities such as schools and hospitals.

Dolphin made a second landfall at Yueqing in Zhejiang at about 6.40pm on Sunday, roughly an hour after first coming ashore at Yuhuan in Taizhou. The twin Zhejiang hits left coastal and inland districts dealing with flood damage just as central agencies began releasing recovery cash.

The Ministry of Water Resources has kept a Level-III emergency response in Zhejiang and a Level-IV response for six other provincial-level regions across eastern, central and northeastern China. Local authorities have been told to strengthen monitoring, step up hazard patrols and give priority to flood defence on small and medium-sized rivers and to mountain-torrent prevention.

Separately on Monday, the Ministry of Finance and the Ministry of Emergency Management allocated 180 million yuan from the central natural disaster relief fund. Of that, 120 million yuan went to Jiangsu, Zhejiang, Anhui, Fujian and Jiangxi for emergency response and rescue after flooding and typhoon damage, while the rest went to Inner Mongolia and Xinjiang to ease ongoing drought.

The National Meteorological Center forecast that from 8pm Monday to 8pm Tuesday, parts of Zhejiang, Jiangsu, Anhui, Henan and Hubei would see torrential rain, with extraordinarily heavy rainstorms possible in southern Henan and northwestern Hubei. After Tuesday, Dolphin's forward speed is expected to slow sharply, but its remnant vortex may linger over the Hubei-Henan-Anhui border and trigger another regional downpour.

With the rain belt shifting north, the centre also warned that from 11th to 13th August the Beijing-Tianjin-Hebei region would face heavy rainfall, and that Beijing itself could see heavy to torrential rain in that window. The dual funding packages and raised water alerts therefore cover both Sunday's coastal landfalls and a multi-day inland rain threat still unfolding.
