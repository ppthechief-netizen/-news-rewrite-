10th August 2026 - (Windhoek) Standard Bank Namibia on Monday launched China's Cross-Border Interbank Payment System, becoming the first bank in the country to offer the service for direct renminbi transactions aimed at boosting bilateral trade and investment.

CIPS provides clearing and settlement for cross-border RMB payments and trade. The bank said the service could cut settlement processing times to about 35 minutes, lower transaction costs and reduce reliance on intermediary currencies that often slow China-corridor deals.

Chief executive Erwin Tjipuka said demand for efficient cross-border payment tools is growing, with China remaining one of Namibia's most important trading partners. "By bringing CIPS to Namibia, we are giving our clients faster settlement," he said, arguing that fewer intermediaries and lower currency risk create a competitive edge for firms working the China corridor.

Chinese ambassador Zhao Weiping welcomed the launch as an important step in deepening economic and trade cooperation. About 17 per cent of Namibia's cross-border payments currently go to China, and nearly one-third of those are already settled in RMB, he said. "This shows that the foundation for the use of RMB," Zhao added, is becoming increasingly solid in China-Namibia commerce.

Zhao also noted that China has remained Namibia's second-largest trading partner and largest source of foreign direct investment for several consecutive years. The timing matters: the launch follows Namibian president Netumbo Nandi-Ndaitwah's state visit to China in July, when the two sides signed several economic and trade cooperation documents.

The Namibia debut sits on a wider African RMB clearing push. In November 2025, Standard Bank Group became the first on the continent authorised to participate in CIPS. In June, the People's Bank of China jointly authorised Standard Bank Group and ICBC to clear RMB transactions in 19 African countries, with the two banks operating as the Renminbi Clearing Bank of Africa.

For Namibian importers, exporters and investors, a local CIPS hook-up is meant to shorten the chain between Windhoek and Chinese counterparties, trimming the need to route every payment through third currencies. Officials framed Monday's start as both a banking product launch and a signal that RMB settlement is moving from a niche option toward a more routine channel in one of southern Africa's key resource and investment relationships with China.
