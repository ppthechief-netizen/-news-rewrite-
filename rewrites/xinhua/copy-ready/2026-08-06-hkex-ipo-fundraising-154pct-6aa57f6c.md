10th August 2026 - (Hong Kong) The Hong Kong Exchanges and Clearing Limited said on Thursday that funds raised through initial public offerings in the first seven months of 2026 reached 328.2 billion Hong Kong dollars, about 41.8 billion US dollars, up 154 per cent from the same period a year earlier.

The IPO haul marks a sharp rebound in primary-market appetite on the city’s main listing venue and puts fundraising well ahead of last year’s comparable stretch. Exchange officials presented the figure as evidence that issuers and investors have returned in force, with the seven-month total already reflecting a deep pipeline of capital-raising rather than a single blockbuster deal.

Listing activity kept pace with the fundraising surge. Data showed the HKEX recorded 104 new listings in the first six months of 2026, a year-on-year increase of 96 per cent. That near-doubling of debuts suggests the fundraising jump was broad-based across a larger cohort of newcomers, not merely a handful of oversized offerings dominating the totals.

Secondary-market turnover also strengthened. Average daily turnover in July stood at 307.2 billion Hong Kong dollars, up 17 per cent year on year. For the first seven months as a whole, average daily turnover climbed 18 per cent to 286.8 billion Hong Kong dollars, according to the HKEX, pointing to sustained trading interest alongside the primary-market revival.

By the end of July, the exchange had 2,763 listed companies with a total market capitalisation of about 46.8 trillion Hong Kong dollars. The combination of higher IPO proceeds, almost twice as many first-half listings, firmer July liquidity and a multi-trillion market base sketches a market that has expanded on both the issuance and trading fronts through mid-2026.

Taken together, the HKEX release portrays Hong Kong’s equity platform as recovering fundraising depth while keeping daily liquidity on an upward path. The 154 per cent leap in IPO funds, the 96 per cent rise in first-half listings and the double-digit gains in average daily turnover form a consistent set of metrics for the first seven months of the year.
