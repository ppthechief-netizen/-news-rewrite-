10th August 2026 - (Beijing) China's Ministry of Commerce issued a preliminary ruling on Monday in its anti-dumping investigation into pecan imports from Mexico and the United States, finding that the nuts were dumped in the Chinese market and caused material injury to domestic producers.

The probe opened on 25th September 2025. The ministry said it conducted the review under China's anti-dumping laws and World Trade Organization rules, adhering to principles of fairness, impartiality, openness and transparency throughout the process. That procedural account matters because provisional duties rest on an investigation still moving toward a final determination rather than on a closed case.

Following the preliminary findings, the ministry decided to impose provisional anti-dumping measures. Importers will face the published margins while the case proceeds, and domestic producers secured a preliminary finding that foreign pecans entered the market at dumped prices and hurt local industry. For Mexican companies, it set dumping margins ranging from 17.8 per cent to 51.6 per cent, reflecting company-specific participation in the probe. No US companies took part in the investigation, so the ministry applied a dumping margin of 54.3 per cent to all American firms, in line with Chinese law and WTO rules. That single-country rate applies across US exporters because none submitted evidence within the investigation framework.

In a separate statement, the ministry said China exercises prudence and restraint in deploying trade remedy measures and remains committed to fair and free trade. That framing accompanied the provisional duties rather than replacing them, signalling that Beijing views the pecan case as a lawful response to injury findings while still stressing broader support for open commerce.

The ministry added that it will continue the investigation in accordance with the law, fully safeguard the rights of all interested parties, and issue an objective and fair final ruling based on the investigation results. Until that final decision, the provisional margins will govern imports of pecans from Mexico and the United States into China.
