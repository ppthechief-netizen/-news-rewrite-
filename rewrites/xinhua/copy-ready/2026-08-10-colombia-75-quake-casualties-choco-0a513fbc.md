10th August 2026 - (Bogota) A powerful earthquake struck western Colombia on Monday, with China's earthquake authority measuring the shock at magnitude 7.5 and the Pacific province of Choco reporting casualties and significant damage.

The China Earthquake Networks Center said the quake jolted Colombia at 8.34pm on Monday, Beijing time. Monitors placed the epicentre at 4.85 degrees north latitude and 76.15 degrees west longitude, with a focal depth of 80 kilometres. Those coordinates put the source in the country's west, near the Pacific margin that includes Choco and neighbouring highland and coastal districts.

Germany's GFZ Research Centre for Geosciences earlier listed a magnitude of 6.8 for a Colombia shock at 1234 GMT on Monday, with a preliminary epicentre near 4.86 degrees north and 76.19 degrees west and a depth of about 93.7 kilometres. The two readings sit close in place and time, illustrating how early magnitude and depth estimates can differ between agencies as waveforms are processed.

Choco's governor said on X that the Monday morning quake caused casualties and significant damage in the Pacific province. The short official message did not immediately list a full death toll, hospital tallies or a municipality-by-municipality damage map, but it confirmed that the human and physical impact had already reached provincial leadership.

Western Colombia's Pacific belt is earthquake-prone, and deeper shocks of the sort described in the Chinese bulletin — tens of kilometres below the surface — can still shake towns across a wide radius. Residents and local officials typically face a scramble for information in the first hours: checking bridges and slopes, clearing debris and accounting for people in informal hillside homes that are vulnerable when the ground moves.

As of the early advisories, investigators and provincial authorities were still compiling the fuller picture of who was hurt and which buildings, roads or utilities failed. Further updates from Colombian civil defence and international seismological services are likely to refine both the magnitude consensus and the casualty count as search and damage surveys continue through Choco and nearby areas.
