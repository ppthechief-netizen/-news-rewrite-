10th August 2026 - (Beijing) China's Ministry of Finance and Ministry of Emergency Management on Monday allocated 180 million yuan (about 26.52 million US dollars) from the central natural disaster relief fund to support disaster-hit areas across the country.

The move comes as eastern China deals with torrential rain and severe flooding caused by Typhoon Dolphin, the 13th typhoon of the year, while parts of northwestern China continue to grapple with prolonged drought. Beijing's disbursement therefore addresses two distinct weather emergencies unfolding at the same time — excess rainfall and flood damage in the east, and persistent dry conditions in the northwest.

Of the total, 120 million yuan was earmarked for five provinces — Jiangsu, Zhejiang, Anhui, Fujian and Jiangxi — to support emergency response and rescue efforts following the flooding and typhoon damage linked to Typhoon Dolphin. The eastern allocation is the larger share of the package, reflecting the immediate rescue and recovery burden created by torrential rain and severe flooding along the affected seaboard and adjacent inland areas.

The funds will mainly cover search and rescue operations, evacuation and temporary shelter of displaced residents, hazard mitigation and urgent risk removal, safety inspections to prevent secondary disasters, and repairs to damaged and collapsed homes. That list spans front-line life-saving work, short-term housing for people forced from their properties, and follow-on steps to reduce further harm and begin rebuilding where structures failed.

The remaining 60 million yuan was allocated to Inner Mongolia, Xinjiang and the Xinjiang Production and Construction Corps to address ongoing drought conditions. That funding will be used primarily to ensure drinking water access for urban and rural residents, including the purchase and rental of emergency equipment for water storage, purification and supply, and the deployment of personnel to secure household water supplies for affected communities.

The two ministries urged local authorities to use the funds efficiently and transparently to maximise their impact on protecting lives and livelihoods. By routing 180 million yuan through the central natural disaster relief fund and splitting it between flood response in five eastern provinces and drought relief in northwestern areas, Beijing aimed to channel resources directly into rescue, shelter, hazard control and water security on both fronts.
