10th August 2026 - (Hong Kong) Hong Kong’s temperature had climbed to 36.9 degrees Celsius by 3.30pm on Sunday, the highest mark since the city began keeping meteorological records in 1884, the Hong Kong Observatory said.

The reading landed in the middle of a fierce late-summer spell that drove people onto beaches and into the sea as the mercury pushed past ordinary heatwave thresholds. Officials framed the spike not as a one-hour quirk but as the culmination of a meteorological pattern already gripping the territory, with the Observatory linking the extreme warmth to broader regional weather rather than a purely local microclimate.

According to the Observatory, the blazing conditions stemmed from subsiding air on the periphery of Typhoon Dolphin. That descending air can suppress cloud and intensify surface heating, helping temperatures surge even when the storm’s core is far from Hong Kong’s coastline. The agency’s account placed the city on the edge of the typhoon’s circulation rather than under its heaviest rain bands, a setup that often brings clear skies and oppressive heat to southern China.

Forecasters expect the sweltering weather to linger for a couple of days. In some areas, they said, temperatures could top 37 degrees Celsius, raising the prospect of another record challenge if the same subsidence pattern holds. Residents were left facing continued heat stress across outdoor work, commuting and recreation while the Observatory tracked whether the ceiling would break again.

Typhoon Dolphin itself made landfall in Zhejiang Province on China’s eastern coast on Sunday afternoon, underlining how a storm far up the mainland seaboard can still shape Hong Kong’s thermometer through peripheral dynamics. The Observatory’s timeline therefore ties a local extreme — 36.9C by mid-afternoon — to a regional cyclone event whose landfall was hundreds of kilometres away, yet close enough in atmospheric terms to drive record heat over the harbour city.

Beach and swimming scenes across Hong Kong on Sunday illustrated how people sought relief even as the mercury climbed to a figure unmatched in more than 140 years of official observation. With the Observatory warning that the heat may persist and could exceed 37C locally, the record stands as both a historical marker and an immediate public-weather alert for the days ahead.
