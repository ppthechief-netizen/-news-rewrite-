10th August 2026 - (Beijing) China's Ministry of Water Resources activated a Level-IV emergency flood response for four provincial regions on Monday, as heavy rainfall brought about by Typhoon Dolphin and a cold air mass is expected in central and northern parts of the country.

The four provinces of Hubei, Henan, Hebei and Shandong will see downpours from 10th August to 16th August amid the 13th typhoon of 2026, with heavy rainstorms expected in some areas, according to forecasts. The window covers nearly a week of unsettled weather rather than a single storm day, raising the risk of cumulative runoff on rivers and slopes already under seasonal pressure.

To cope with the situation, the ministry has instructed local authorities to strengthen monitoring and research efforts and step up patrols to detect and address potential hazards. Officials are being told to treat the coming rains as an operational test of local flood defences, not merely a weather advisory, with patrols aimed at finding weak points before water levels rise.

Priority will be given to flood defence along small and medium-sized rivers and the prevention of mountain torrents, according to the ministry. It also stressed the timely evacuation of residents in high-risk areas to ensure the safety of people's lives and property. That mix of riverine defence, torrent control and early evacuation reflects the typical hazard pattern when typhoon moisture meets inland terrain and cooler air.

Meanwhile, the ministry maintained a Level-III emergency response in eastern China's Zhejiang Province, as well as a Level-IV response for six other provincial-level regions across eastern, central and northeastern China. Zhejiang therefore remains on a higher footing than the four newly activated provinces, while the six other Level-IV areas keep an existing state of readiness alongside Monday's fresh activation.

The ministry's information center on Monday renewed a blue alert for floods, advising the public across affected areas in northern, central and eastern China to take precautions. China has a four-tier emergency response system, with Level I being the most severe response. It also has a four-tier, colour-coded weather warning system, with red representing the most severe warning, followed by orange, yellow and blue. Monday's blue flood alert therefore sits at the lowest colour tier while the Level-IV response for Hubei, Henan, Hebei and Shandong marks the least severe of the four emergency grades — still a formal mobilisation ahead of the Typhoon Dolphin rain band.
