10th August 2026 - (Hong Kong) An unemployed man who dragged his girlfriend's corpse through Hung Shui Kiu on a hand trolley has been jailed for three years and nine months after a jury convicted him of manslaughter rather than murder.

Ng Ka-sing, 29, had faced a murder charge over the death of Yip Tsz-ching, 30, at their shared flat in Galore Garden on Tin Ha Road between 28th and 29th April 2022. In HCCC340/2024 the panel cleared him of murder but found him guilty of manslaughter and of preventing the lawful burial of a body; he had also offered before trial to admit manslaughter. Mrs Justice Judianna Barnes Wai-ling passed sentence today after obtaining a psychiatric report.

The case surfaced shortly before 6.00am on 29th April 2022 when a jogger and a street cleaner saw Ng pulling a trolley along Tin Ha Road with a human foot protruding from the load. Both alerted police. Ng waited at the scene. When a woman officer asked what had happened, he replied calmly that the wrapped figure was his girlfriend and that he had accidentally beaten her to death with a wooden stick.

Officers found Yip's body inside a quilt, her head bound in cling film. Government pathologist Dr Foo Ka-chung said that, at about 1.59 metres and 64 kilograms, she bore widespread bruises and abrasions, including a large injury at the back of the head. Corrosive burns covered roughly 55 per cent of her skin on the chest, abdomen, limbs and genitals, some severe. Dr Foo said death followed head trauma and extensive chemical burns, then suffocation, and that the burns were unlikely to be self-inflicted. The cleaner Wong Nga-sam said Ng told him the trolley held a corpse and that he meant to take it to a police station; jogger Lau Kwok-yan said a foot had fallen from the bundle before he called officers.

In a recorded interview Ng said Yip's god-sister had ordered a sleepless "weight-loss" regime because she feared Yip was dangerously obese, and instructed him to strike Yip whenever she dozed off. He admitted assaulting her with a stick between 10.00pm and 1.30am on 27th April and again between 3.00am and 5.30am the next morning. He said Yip had poured drain cleaner over herself, complained of pain and asked why she had to suffer, and that he told her skipping meals would spare her further beatings. Around 7.00am on 28th April he found her unresponsive, tried resuscitation, then at about 3.00am on the 29th loaded her onto the trolley intending to surrender.

At trial his account shifted in detail. Ng said that after moving in with the god-sister and her husband, nicknamed Ah Long, rent had risen from HK$5,000 to HK$30,000, he had borrowed from a finance company, and Ah Long had beaten him, barred him from leaving for work and forced the Christian defendant to copy Buddhist scriptures. About a month before the killing the god-sister banned Yip from sleeping or eating properly, allowing only water and vegetables every two days. When Yip was caught eating on 27th April, Ng beat her back with the stick. He said Ah Long later made them play guessing and "pendulum" games, striking their ankles with a mallet and marking their upper backs with cotton buds soaked in drain cleaner; Ng stripped in court to show burn scars.

Ng said he snatched away drain cleaner when Yip doused herself, but beat her again when the god-sister said she still showed no fear, until Yip turned pale. Ah Long then declared her dead. Ng said he cradled the body and apologised, but rejected a suggestion to dismember her and instead wrapped her for the trolley trip. He withdrew an earlier claim that Yip groaned when her head was bound.

Three flatmates who had faced a charge of preventing lawful burial — warehouse worker Chan Wing-hong, 27, housewife Cheng Pui-man, 28, and her husband Tsoi Chi-long, 31 — saw their cases withdrawn at the end of 2023. Prosecutors pursued Ng alone to sentence.
